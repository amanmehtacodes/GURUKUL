                                           Noun : Gender
On the basis of gender, there are four categories of nouns:
  (i) Masculine Gender: Masculine gender refers to a male.
Examples: boy, man, father, tiger, horse etc.
  (ii) Feminine Gender: Feminine gender refers to a female.
Examples: girl, woman, mother, tigress
  (iii) Common Gender: A noun in common gender denotes both a male and a female.
Examples:
            doctor             engineer           child        leader           infant
            singer             enemy              bird         member           thief
            servant            parent             applicant    patient          convict
            orphan             teacher            artist       painter          lawyer
            ﬁsh                student            friend       person           counsellor
            eagle              baby               driver       hunter           boss
(iv) Neuter Gender: Neuter gender denotes neither a male nor a female. It is used for non-living things. The
pronoun used for a neuter gender is ‘it’. Generally, material nouns, singular collective nouns and abstract nouns
are considered to be in neuter gender.
Examples: table, pencil, box, book, car etc.

    Some Important Rules of Nouns : Numbers and Verbs used for them
 1. Some nouns are always used as plural nouns. They are used with plural verbs.
 Examples: alms, amends, annals, earnings, scissors, trousers, pants, clippers, socks, fetters, eyeglasses, riches,
 entrails, remains, spectacles, tidings, thanks, tongs, vegetables, bowels etc.
 2. Some nouns appear to be plural, but are used as singular nouns. They are used with singular verbs.
 Examples: mathematics, statistics, physics, economics, politics, innings, news, classics, gymnastics, summons,
 mumps, billiards, shambles etc.
 3. Some nouns appear to be singular, but are used as plural. They are used with plural verbs.
 Examples: cattle, clergy, cavalry, infantry, peasantry, gentry, police, folk, people, public, vermin, poultry etc.
 4. Some nouns are used only as singular uncountable nouns. They are used with singular verbs.
 Examples: scenery, poetry, furniture, advice, information, business, mischief, jewellery, breakage, knowledge,
 wastage, money, bread, crockery, luggage, rice, fuel, offspring, etc.
 5. Some nouns have the same forms in singular and plural.
 Examples: deer, ﬁsh, crew, jury, aircraft, counsel, apparatus, cod, yolk, species, means, series etc.
 6. Some nouns express length, measure, money, weight, number or counting. When preceded by a numeral,
 these nouns can be used in plural but if they are preceded by a numeral and followed by a noun, they
 remain singular in form. Examples: foot, hundred, metre, million, year, score etc.
       (i) We are ﬂying at 35000 feet. (ii) I saw a 6 foot long snake. (iii) Give me two ﬁve rupee notes.
       (iv) Give me ﬁve hundred rupees.
 7. The words ‘pair’ and ‘dozen’ can be used both as singular and plural depending on the number of pairs
 or dozens.
 Example: I have a pair of socks.
              I have two pairs of socks.
              He bought two dozen of oranges.
              He bought dozens of oranges.
 8. Some nouns are both countable and uncountable depending on their meaning in a sentence.
 Example: I don’t have much time. (Uncountable)
              I have told you many times not to disturb me. (Countable)

                                                               Noun : Case
Nouns function in various ways in sentences.
1. Nouns are subjects. Every sentence has a subject, which is a noun that tells us what that sentence is all about.
      Examples: 1. John swung the baseball bat. 2. Harry wrote a letter.
2. Nouns are direct objects. These nouns receive action from verbs.
      Examples: 1. John swung the baseball bat. 2. Harry wrote a letter.
3. Nouns are indirect objects. These nouns receive the direct object.
      Examples: 1. Brad threw John the ball. 2. My father bought my sister a bicycle.
4. Nouns are objects of prepositions. These nouns follow the prepositions in prepositional phrases.
      Examples: 1. John swung the baseball bat at Greg. 2. Rehan kept his books on the shelf.
5. Nouns are subject complements. These nouns follow linking verbs and rename the subject.
      Examples: 1. John is a baseball player. 2. Radhika is a singer.
6. Nouns are object complements. These nouns complete the direct object.
      Examples: 1. They named their dog Max.         2. The coach appointed Rohan captain.
7. Nouns are nominative of address. These nouns are used to address a person, animal or thing.
      Examples: 1. Friends, follow me.       2. Mohan, come here. 3. Sit down, boys.
8. Nouns are used in apposition. These nouns follow another noun to denote the same person or thing.
      Examples: 1. Rohan, my brother, is a cricketer. 2. Mr. Sharma, our neighbour, is a gentleman.

                                                      Noun : Possessive Case
The possessive case of a noun is formed using the sign of apostrophe (’). Apostrophe is used to form the
possessive case of noun to show a sense of belonging or ownership or some relationship between a noun and
another noun.
Examples: Mohan’s book (The book belongs to Mohan.)
          Horse’s tail (The tail belongs to horse.)

                             Correct Use of Apostrophe (’)

1. When the noun is singular, the possessive case is formed by adding -’s to the noun.
Examples: Punit’s book, the boy’s pen
2. When the noun is plural and ends with ‘s’, the possessive case is formed by adding only an apostrophe.
Examples: girls’ hostel, boys’ school
3. When the noun is plural, but does not end with ‘s’, the possessive case is formed by adding -’s.
Examples: children’s day, men’s store
4. If there are too many hissing sounds in a word, the letter ‘s’ is omitted after the apostrophe.
Examples: for conscience’ sake, for goodness’ sake, Moses’ law
5. When a noun or a title consists of many words, the possessive case is formed by adding -’s to the last word.
Example: the queen of England’s palace
6. When two nouns are in apposition, -’s is added only to the latter.
Example: This is Raju, the painter’s house.
7. When two nouns are closely linked, the possessive case is formed by adding -’s to the latter. This is known as
joint possession.
Examples: Sohan and Mohan’s book, Wren and Martin’s Grammar
8. If two nouns are connected, and they show separate possession, the -’s will be used with both of them.
Example: Milton’s and Browning’s poems
9. Classical names ending with ‘s’ usually take only apostrophe.
Examples: Pythagoras’ theorem, Archimedes’ law
10. The possessive is not used with inanimate/non-living objects.
Example: the table’s leg (incorrect), leg of table (correct)
              the house’s roof (incorrect), the roof of the house (correct)
11. If an inanimate object is personiﬁed, the possessive case is formed by adding -’s.
Examples: India’s heroes, nature’s law, fortune’s wheel
12. Possessive case is used with nouns indicating time, space, weight in idiomatic expressions.
Examples: a day’s rest, a week’s holiday, in a year’s time, at a stone’s throw, on wit’s end, a hair’s breadth etc.
13. In a compound noun, the possessive case is formed by adding -’s to the last part.
Examples: my mother-in-law’s books, the editor-in-chief’s car
14. When a proper noun or name takes -’s to denote an institution or organization, the nouns referring to these
institutions and organizations are omitted.
Examples: I am going to St. Joseph’s (St. Joseph’s school)
15. Use of double possessive should be avoided.
Example: My father’s friend’s house (incorrect)
             The house of my father’s friend (correct)
