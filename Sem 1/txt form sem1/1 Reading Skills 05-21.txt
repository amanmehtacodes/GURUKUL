                                             Reading Skills
Reading is a complex "cognitive process" of decoding symbols in order to construct or derive meaning (reading
comprehension). When we read, we use our eyes to receive written symbols (letters, punctuation marks and
spaces) and we use our brain to convert them into words, sentences and paragraphs that communicate something
to us.
Reading is a means of language acquisition, communication, and of sharing information and ideas. Like all
languages, it is a complex interaction between the text and the reader which is shaped by the reader's prior
knowledge, experiences, attitude, and language community which is culturally and socially situated. The reading
process requires continuous practice, development, and reﬁnement. In addition, reading requires creativity and
critical analysis. Reading can be silent (in our head) or aloud (so that other people can hear). Reading is a
receptive skill - through it we receive information. But the complex process of reading also requires the skill of
speaking, so that we can pronounce the words that we read. In this sense, reading is also a productive skill in that
we are both receiving information and transmitting it.

The two most important sub skills of reading are:
   1. Skimming: 'Skimming' a text means going through it quickly to get an overall idea of the content. We are
       not interested in details or any speciﬁc information while skimming.
   2. Scanning: 'Scanning' on the other hand, involves searching the text for speciﬁc piece of information in
       which the reader is interested. For example ﬁnding a particular name or date given in the passage.
                                       How to Read Effectively
   1. Remain calm and cool while reading and read the text silently and very carefully.

   2. Move from skimming to scanning. Go from the 'whole' to the 'parts', and not vice versa.

   3. Don't pick up information from the text in small bits and pieces, try to read complete clauses or sentences

       in one go to understand the text well.
   4. Don't get scared if you don't know the meaning of a particular word. Try to guess the meaning of the word

       in the context in which it is used.
   5. Keep noting or underlining important points, ideas, names, deﬁnitions, days or dates in the passage.

   6. Pay special attention to cohesive devices or discourse markers because these are 'signposts' provided by

       the writer. These are used in a text to indicate sequence of ideas and signal the writer's point of view.
       Understanding the writer's use of discourse markers is an important sub-skill of reading. These signposts
       are helpful because they indicate to the reader the relationship between two parts of the text. They also
       indicate the writer's opinions, mood or arguments.
   7. Don't skip words or lines while reading as you may miss some important ideas.
                                                        05
   8. The reading comprehension questions are generally asked in the sequence in which the text is arranged.

       However, be careful while you answer the questions.
   9. The questions may be based on facts, information, overall understanding, reasons, causes, effects, merits,

       demerits, types, deﬁnitions, functions, purpose or inference. Read the question very carefully and
       understand it before looking for the answer.
   10. Try to answer the questions in indirect speech making required changes in pronouns, verbs and tenses

       using correct connectors.
   11. Answer all the questions based on the reading passage given to you. Don't add your own information or

       opinion until you are asked to do so.


               Types of questions based on reading comprehension

(i) Multiple Choice Questions: In this category the students will be provided options to choose their answer
from.
(ii) Blanks: In this category the students will be required to ﬁll in the blanks by collecting information from the
passage.
(iii) True/False: In this category the students will be required to write 'true' or 'false' in front of the given
sentences based on their understanding of the given text.
(iv) Matching: In this category the students will be required to match the words/phrases given in the columns
according to a particular criterion.
(v) Very Short Answers: In this category the students will be required to answer the questions in one word or a
few words.
(vi) Short Answers: In this category the students will be required to answer the questions in one or two
sentences.
(vii) Arranging the sentences: In this category the students may be required to arrange the given sentences in the
sequence in which they appear in the text.
(viii) Mind Maps or Flow Charts: In this category the students may be required to complete the mind maps or
ﬂow charts by writing the required information.

                         Literary Terms or Figures of Speech
Literary terms describe the various techniques that writers use to create a particular emotional effect on the
readers' mind. It is use of literary terms that gives a special meaning to a piece of text be it prose or poetry. It
beautiﬁes the language and adds to its charm. Here are some common literary terms.
1. A simile is a ﬁgure of speech that directly compares two things. Although similes and metaphors are similar,
    similes explicitly use connecting words such as like, as, so, than, or various verbs such as resemble. Example:
    “O My Love's like a red, red rose." "A Red, Red Rose," by Robert Burns.
2. A metaphor is a ﬁgure of speech that refers to something as being the same as another thing for rhetorical
    effect. It may provide clarity or identify hidden similarities between two ideas. Whereas a simile compares
    two items, a metaphor directly equates them, and does not use "like" or "as" as does a simile. Example:
    “All the world's a stage,
    And all the men and women merely players;
    They have their exits and their entrances[...]”                          —William Shakespeare, As You Like It
3. Alliteration is the repetition of initial sounds in neighbouring words. Example: “She sells sea shells on the sea
    shore.” The other two ﬁgures of speech related to repetition of sounds are consonance and assonance.
    consonance is the repetition of a consonant sound at any position in two or more words whereas assonance is
    the repetition of a vowel sound in two or more words.
4. Refrain is a line or group of lines that repeats at regular intervals, in different stanzas. It not only gives a
    rhythmic effect to the poem but also emphasises the central idea of the poem.
5. Personiﬁcation is a ﬁgure of speech where human qualities are given to animals, objects or ideas. Example:
    The leaves were dancing in the rain.
6. Onomatopoeia is a word that phonetically imitates, resembles or suggests the source of the sound that it
    describes. Onomatopoeia (as an uncountable noun) refers to the property of such words. Common
    occurrences of onomatopoeias include animal noises such as, "miaow", "roar" or "chirp". Examples: tinkling
    bells or rustling of silk etc.
7. Irony, in its broadest sense, is a rhetorical device, literary technique, or event in which what appears, on the
    surface, to be the case, differs radically from what is actually the case. Irony may be divided into categories
    such as verbal, dramatic, and situational. In City Lights the audience knows that Charlie Chaplin's character is
    not a millionaire, but the blind ﬂower girl (Virginia Cherrill) believes him to be rich.
8. A transferred epithet is a ﬁgure of speech in which an epithet (or adjective) grammatically qualiﬁes a noun
    other than the person or thing it is actually describing. A transferred epithet often involves shifting a modiﬁer
    from the animate to the inanimate, as in the phrases "cheerful money," "sleepless night," and "suicidal sky.”
9. Pun, also called paronomasia, is a form of word play that suggests two or more meanings, by exploiting
    multiple meanings of words, or of similar-sounding words, for an intend. Examples: (i) A bicycle can't stand
    on its own because it is two-tired. (ii) No matter how much you push the envelope, it will still be stationery.
10.Oxymoron is a ﬁgure of speech in which two opposite ideas are joined to create an effect. The common
    oxymoron phrase is a combination of an adjective proceeded by a noun with contrasting meanings. Examples:
    “cruel kindness” or “living death” “Tragic comedy” etc.
11. A Paradox is a self contradictory statement that contains two opposite facts. Example: Success is precious for
    the people who never succeed.

12. Allusion is an indirect reference to a person, event, or thing in history or literature.
13. Hyperbole is a ﬁgure of speech in which the writer exaggerates and makes something sound bigger or more
   impressive than it really is. Example: Her eyes were deeper than the ocean.
14. Imagery: When a writer uses words to create a picture in your mind, it is called imagery. There are various
   types of imagery. Many of them are related to our ﬁve senses which help us create the mental image of the
   thing we are reading about. Visual Imagery creates sight experience; olfactory imagery, smell; auditory
   imagery, sound; gustatory imagery, taste; and kinaesthetic imagery, touch.

                                           We Who Love Books
                                      Some books I’ll never give away,
                                   Though old and worn, their binding torn,
                                     Upon my shelves they’ll always stay,
                                    Alive, still read, still fresh each dawn,
                                      Their magic moments never gone.

                                     Familiar friends, these timeless tales
                                      Have been with me since I was ten,
                                     And as I turn their pages once again
                                       I feel and love their old refrain.

                              Great verse, great thoughts, still stand the test
                              Of time that’s passing by so fast......................
                                    These good companions never fail
                                      To give me joy, to nourish me.
                                    We who love books will always be
                                    The lucky ones, our minds set free.
                                                                                          Ruskin Bond
