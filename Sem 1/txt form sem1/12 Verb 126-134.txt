 Chapter-11                                      The Verb
There are two major parts of a sentence: Subject and Predicate. Subject is the person or thing we talk about in a
sentence. Predicate is the statement given about the subject. A predicate always starts with a verb.
Study the following table carefully.
                   Sr.      Subject                            Predicate
                   No.                      Verb         Remaining part of the Predicate
                   1       Children     are playing      in the park.
                   2       Rakesh       is writing       a letter.
                   3       Surbhi       played           with her doll.
                   4       India        is               a great country.
The word ‘verb’ has been taken from the Latin word ‘verbum’ which means ‘to say something’.

Deﬁnition: A verb is a word that says something about a subject. A verb may tell three things about a
subject.
1. It tells us what a subject(s) is. It denotes the existence of a subject in any tense. The forms of ‘be’ are
   used to denote existence.
                 Forms of ‘be’            be, is, am, are, was, were, been, being
(a) He is my friend.                  (b) She was a doctor.               (c) They were thieves.
(d)You have been a fool.              (e) You will be happy.              (f) I am very happy today.

Here the verbs ‘is’, ‘was’ ‘were’ ‘been’ ‘be’ and ‘am’ denote existence.
2. It tells us what a subject(s) has/owns/possesses. The forms of ‘have’ are used to denote possession.
  The possession may be denoted in any tense.

                 Forms of ‘have’          has, have, had, having
                   (a) I have a car.                     (c) They will have a problem.
                   (b) She has a big house.              (d) The boy had a blue shirt.
Here the verbs ‘have’, ‘has’, ‘will have’ and ‘had’ denote possessions.
3. It tells us what a subject(s) does. All action words are verbs. The action may be expressed in any
  tense.
      (a) Harish waters the plants.      (b) The peon rang the bell.
      (c) She is laughing.               (d) They have been playing football since morning.

Here, the verbs ‘waters’, ‘rang’, ‘laughing ’ and ‘playing’ denote actions.

Study the following table carefully.

A Noun or a pronouns that receives the action of the verb is called the object. A verb may take more than one
object.

There are three types of verbs.
  u Intransitive Verbs
  u Transitive Verbs
  u Linking Verbs or Verbs of Incomplete Predication
                                                           Intransitive Verbs
Study the table given below carefully:
                                       Subject             Verb
                                       Birds               ﬂy.
                                       Stars               twinkle.
                                       She                 laughs.
                                       They                smiled.
                                       The windows         broke.
                                       The boy             was sitting.
The verbs ‘ﬂy’, ‘twinkle’, ‘laughs’ ‘smiled’ ‘broke’ and ‘was sitting’ make a complete sense on their own. They
don't require any other word to complete their meaning. Since the action denoted by each of these verbs does not
pass over to any other word (object), these verbs are called intransitive verbs.
Deﬁnition:- An intransitive verb does not take any object after it.
                                                             Transitive Verbs
Important Note: The word ‘transitive’ is derived from the word ‘transit’ which means 'to pass over' or 'transfer'.
Read the following sentences carefully. You can observe that these sentences seem incomplete unless we
add an object to them.

   He killed........................................ (What/whom)               We have informed...................................... (Whom)
   They were helping.................................. (Whom)                  Children like................................................. (What)
   I am reading.............................................. (What)           She teaches.......................................(What/ Whom)
   Open......................................................... (What)        I have completed........................................... (What)
   Karim beats............................................ (Whom)              She stitched.................................................. (What)

In the above sentences, the verbs don't make a complete sense themselves. They require some other words to
complete their meaning. The action denoted by each verb in these sentences passes over to an object. Therefore,
these verbs are called transitive verbs.
Deﬁnition:- A transitive verb takes an object after it.

        They were helping the injured.                  Children like sweets.

Some transitive verbs don't make a complete sense even when they are supplied with an object. They
require an object complement to complete their meaning. An object complement refers back to the object.
Examples: 1. The teacher appointed Mohit...........................................(what)
              2. Success made him.............................................................(what)

In these sentences the underlined words complete the sentences and refer back to the subject. There is no transfer
of action. Hence, these words are called subject complements. Since the verbs in these sentences establish a link
between the subject and the subject complement, they are called linking verbs or verbs of incomplete predication.
The forms of ‘be’ used as main verbs and verbs like ‘look’, ‘appear’, ‘seem’, ‘grow’, ‘become’, ‘turn’ etc. take a
subject complement.

                     Main Verbs and Auxiliaries (Helping Verbs)
Read the following sentences and observe the verbs carefully.
    1. I talked to my friend.
    2. I was talking to my friend.
    3. We water the plants.
    4. We have watered the plants.
In the above sentences, the verbs talked, talking, water and watered are called main verbs as they express the
main action. The verbs 'was' and 'have' in the above sentences are also verbs, but these verbs are called helping
verbs because they help the main verbs.

verbs or weak verbs.
