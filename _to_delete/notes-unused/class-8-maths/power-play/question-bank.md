Astronomers prefer light-years because expressing this distance in km (4×10¹³ km) requires unwieldy, hard-to-compare large exponents, whereas "4.4 light years" is immediately meaningful — this mirrors the chapter's point that **the exponent (order of magnitude) is what truly matters for comprehension**, and choosing an appropriate unit (like light-years) effectively "resets" the exponent to a manageable small number.

**Q148.** First 6 months (doubling from 10): 10×2⁶=640. Next 6 months (linear, +500/month): 640+500×6=640+3000=**3640 total users** at the end of 12 months. This is NOT a simple power of 2 over the full year because the growth PATTERN changed halfway through — exponential growth only applies to the first phase, and the second phase is purely additive, so no single exponential expression (like 10×2¹²) correctly captures the full 12-month trajectory; the two phases must be calculated separately and then combined.

**Q149.** Total possible numbers = 10⁶ = 1,000,000. With 100 tickets (100 different numbers) out of these, and only 1 winning number: probability = 100/10⁶ = **1/10⁴ = 1/10,000** (or 10⁻⁴).

**Q150.** _(Open-ended — sample answer:)_ "A hospital lab has a bacteria sample starting at 5×10² cells, which doubles every hour. After 8 hours of doubling, the lab dilutes the sample by a factor of 10⁻³ for testing. Find the final cell count in the diluted sample, expressed in scientific notation." **Solution:** After 8 hours: 5×10²×2⁸ = 5×10²×256 = 1280×10² = 1.28×10⁵ cells. After dilution: 1.28×10⁵×10⁻³ = **1.28×10² cells** (128 cells). _(This example uses TWO exponent rules: repeated doubling as 2⁸, and multiplying by a negative power of 10 for dilution — with the final answer correctly expressed in scientific notation. Any well-constructed original problem meeting both criteria should be accepted.)_
