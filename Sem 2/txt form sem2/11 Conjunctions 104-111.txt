Chapter-10                             The Conjunction
 Study carefully how the following pairs of sentences are joined.
    1. He bought apples. He bought mangoes.
        He bought apples and mangoes.
    2. My brother is tall. I am short.
        My brother is tall but I am short.
    3. He is ill. He cannot come to school
        He is ill, so he cannot come to school.
        He cannot come to school because he is ill.
 You can observe that in each pair of sentences given above, two different statements are given. These two
 different statements are joined by using the words 'and', 'but', 'so' and 'because'. These words which join two
 words or clauses are called conjunctions, connectors or linkers.
 Conjunction: A word which is used to join two words, phrases or clauses is called a conjunction.
                                   Kinds of Conjunction
 1. Coordinative Conjunctions: A Coordinative Conjunction joins the grammatical units of the same type
 or equal status e.g. nouns with noun, adjective with adjective, phrase with phrase and clause with clause. It
 joins words, phrases or clauses of equal status. In a compound Sentence, it joins two coordinate Clauses.
 Examples: and, but, for, or, still, yet, so, therefore, as well as, otherwise, else, only etc.

    s   I went home. I changed my clothes.
        I went home and changed my clothes. (Joins verb with verb)
    s   Take care of your health. You will fall ill.
        Take care of your health, otherwise you will fall ill. (Joins two coordinate clauses)
    s   He likes ice-cream. He does not like juice.
        He likes ice-cream, but not juice. (Joins noun with noun)

 2. Subordinating Conjunction: A subordinating conjunction joins subordinate clause/clauses to the
 principal clause in a complex sentence.
 Examples: unless, until, because, since, as, if, whether, before, after, when, while, where, wherever etc.
    s I used to play cricket when I was a student.
    s He ran away because he was afraid.
 The underlined clauses in the above sentences are Subordinate Clauses.
 3. Correlative Conjunctions: Correlative conjunctions also join grammatical units of the same type or
 equal status e.g. nouns with noun, adjective with adjective, phrase with phrase and clause with clause.
 Correlative conjunctions are followed by the same part of speech. They are always used in pairs.
 Examples: Both..........and, Either..........or, Neither.............nor, Hardly..............when,
 Scarcely........before, Not only..........but also etc.
    s He is either a fool (noun) or a rogue (noun).
    s Hardly had he reached school (clause) when the peon rang the bell. (clause)
 4. Compound Conjunctions: Compound conjunctions are groups of words that function as conjunctions.
 Some expressions or phrases act as conjunctions.
    s He will lend you money provided that you return it on time.
    s I will let you play in my garden on condition that you do not pluck the ﬂowers.
    s The thief ran away as soon as he heard a noise.
 These expressions or phrases are called Compound Conjunctions.

                           Types of Coordinative Conjunctions
1. Cumulative or Copulative Conjunctions: Cumulative conjunctions add or connect words, phrases or
clauses of the same type, status or level. They are used to convey the idea of similarity between the units being
joined. Examples: and, also, too, as well as, both...........and, not only.............but also etc.
Examples: Read the pair of sentences given below.
He is smart. He is intelligent.
In the above pair of sentences, we come to know about two qualities of 'He'. Since both the qualities are positive
and don't show any contradiction, we will join the above pair of sentences using a cumulative conjunction.
Examples:
        1. He is smart and intelligent.               4. He is both smart and intelligent.
        2. He is smart as well as intelligent.        5. He is smart; and intelligent also.
        3. He is smart; and intelligent too.          6. He is not only smart but also intelligent.
2. Adversative Conjunctions: These conjunctions are used to connect or join two contradictory, contrasting
or opposite ideas. Examples: but, still, however, nevertheless, only, whereas, yet
Example: Read the pair of sentences given below.
        He is intelligent. He is not hard working.
        He is intelligent, but not hardworking.
Examples:
            · He is poor but honest.
            · He was weak; still he managed to walk for long.
            · He lost everything in life; however he started his life anew with a strong will.
            · Everyone in the family opposed him; nevertheless he chased his dreams.
            · Do what you like, only don't disturb me.
            · My friend is tall whereas his brother is not (tall).
            · He has a lot of wealth, yet he is a miser.

3. Disjunctive or Alternative Conjunction: These conjunctions express a choice between two words, phrases
or clauses. Examples: or, nor, either..............or, neither...............nor, otherwise, else
Examples:
            · Run fast, or you will lose the race.
            · You can either wait or go.
            · He is neither intelligent nor hardworking.
            · Work hard, otherwise you will fail.
            · Return these books on time, else you will be ﬁned.

4. Illative Conjunctions: When a fact or statement is inferred or provided from another statement, the two of
them are joined by using an illative coordinating conjunctions. Examples: so, hence, therefore, for, then
Examples:
            · My foot was injured, so I could not walk.
            · It had been raining since morning; hence we could not go out.
            · He is not well today; therefore he cannot come to school.
            · He missed the train, for he was late.
            · We are getting late, let's hurry up then.

Important Note: Remember the Acronym FANBOYS to remember some important conjunctions.
      F= for       A=and, as well as  N=neither......nor     B=but
      O=or, otherwise, else           Y=yet, still           S=so, hence, therefore

                               Types of Subordinating Conjunctions
Subordinating functions may be divided according to their meaning or function.
1. Time: Before, as, after, when, while, since, as soon as, till, until, so long as, as long as, now that, once etc.
Examples:
s Look before you leap.
s I will go to play after I have ﬁnished my work.
s I used to play cricket when I was a student.
s I haven't seen her since she left her job.
s Wait here till I come.
2. Place: Where, wherever, whence
s He will follow you wherever you go.
s I kept the books where you told me to keep.
s They returned whence they had come.
3. Manner or Extent: as, so far as, as if, as though, as far as etc.
s They worked as I had instructed.
s So far as his health is concerned, he is better now.
s As far as his behaviour is concerned, he has shown improvement.
s He walked as if he were lame.
s It sounds as though you had a good time.
4. Cause: Because, since, as
s He died because he could not get proper treatment.
s I will lead this project since you want me to do it.
5. Condition: Provided, provided that, on condition that, if, in case, unless, supposing, h
s You can stand ﬁrst in the class provided you work hard.
s You can stand ﬁrst in the class provided that you work hard.
s You can stand ﬁrst in the class if you work hard.
s You will not stand ﬁrst in the class unless you work hard.
6. Result or Effect: so.....................that
s He is so weak that he cannot walk.
7. Purpose: that, so that, in order that, lest............should, for fear that
s He worked hard so that he might pass the examination.
s He is working hard in order that he may pass the examination.
s Run fast lest you should miss the train.
s We eat that we may live.
8. Contrast or Concession: though, although, notwithstanding, as, even if, whether, however, whatever, while,
when
s Though he is poor, he is honest. He is honest though he is poor.
s Although he is poor, he is honest. He is honest although he is poor.
s You have to eat it whether you like it or not.
s Whatever happens, I will ﬁght till the end.
s While he is good at science, his brother is hopeless.
9. Comparison: than, as
s My brother is taller than I am.
s Ramesh ran faster than Suresh. (did).

                                                Correlative Conjunctions
1. Both................and (Used to join similar things/concepts)
    • He is both a lyricist and composer.
2. Either..................or (Used to choose/select either of two choices)
    • You can take either a bus or a train for Delhi.
3. Neither.....................nor (Used to reject either of the two choices)
    • He is neither a doctor nor a lawyer.
4. Hardly......................when (Used in the sense of ‘as soon as’)
    • Hardly had I stepped out of my house when it started raining.
5. Scarcely..........................when/before (Used in the sense of ‘as soon as’)
    • I scarcely had time to ring the bell before the door opened
    • He had scarcely reached school when the peon rang the bell.
6. Not only......................but also (Used in the sense of ‘and’ or ‘in addition to’)
    • He not only invited me, but also gave me a beautiful gift.
7. No sooner.....................than (Used in the sense of ‘as soon as’)
    • No sooner did the thief see the police than he ran away.
    • No sooner had I reached home than it started raining.
8. Though.................yet (Used to express contrast)
    • Though the baby is weak yet it is active. Or,
    • Though the baby is weak, it is active.
9. Although..................yet (Used to express contrast)
    • Although he worked hard yet he could not succeed. Or,
    • Although he worked hard, he could not succeed.
10. So.........................that (Used to show result or effect)
    • He is so intelligent that he can solve these sums easily.
11. Whether.......................or (Used to join two contrasting ideas)
    • You should work under him, whether you like him or not.
12. Lest........................should (Used in the sense of ‘otherwise’)
    • Walk fast lest you should miss the train.
