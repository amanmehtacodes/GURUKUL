 Chapter-9                                 The Preposition
A preposition (pre-position), as the name suggests, is a word which is placed before a noun or a pronoun to
establish the relation between the noun or pronoun and some other word in a sentence. Thus, a preposition
is always followed by a noun or a pronoun.
                                       Types of Preposition
  1. Simple Preposition: in, of, on, up, with, for, at, out, off, by etc.
  2. Compound Prepositions: without, within, outside, inside, into, etc.
  3. Double Prepositions: Outside of, out of, from out, from behind, from beneath etc.
  4. Participle Prepositions: Notwithstanding, concerning, regarding, considering, etc.
  5. Phrase Prepositions: By means of, by virtue of, on account of, in opposition to, with regard to, for the
          sake of, instead of, on behalf of etc.

Examples:
   s He went to market.

2. The place may be seen as a line.

   s They sat at the ﬁrst desk.
   s They went across the ﬁeld.

   s The farmer is in the ﬁeld.

   s The children are playing in the park.
5. The place may be seen as a volume.
   s He went into the room.
   s Keep these books in the box.
   s They went out of the house.
   s The breeze blew through the trees.
6. Prepositions of Relative Position:
   (a) Above, over, on top of, below, under, underneath and beneath show the relative position of two objects
   vertically.
   (b) Before, in front of, behind, by, beside and after show the relative position of two objects horizontally.
Examples:
   s The boy stood under the tree.
   s He kept the letter under the pillow. (not below the pillow)
   s I feel as if I were on top of the world.
   s There is a temple in front of my house.
   s The little child hid behind a tree.
   s There is a bridge above/over the river.
   s A river ﬂows below the bridge.

                                           Prepositions of Time
Time has two dimensions: Point of time and Period of time
                                                   Use of at
   Æ For point of time, chieﬂy clock time: at 6 o' clock, at noon, at night, at midnight
   Æ For holiday periods: at Christmas, at the weekend,
   Æ For time phrase: at that time, at night, at an early stage, at an early date etc.
                                                 Use of on/in
   Æ ‘On’ is used for days and dates: on Saturday, on November 25, 2017
   Æ ‘In’ is used with the names of month, year, morning, evening and afternoon: in July, in 1985, in the
      morning, in the afternoon, in the evening
                                        Use of for, from........to/till, during
‘For’ is used to denote a period of time, ‘from................to/till’ is used to denote a beginning point of time and a
ﬁnishing point of time, ‘during’ is used to denote all through a period of time.
   Æ I lived in Delhi for two years.
   Æ I worked there from February to/till October.
   Æ He learnt skating during summer vacation.
                                       Use of before, after, since, till/until
These prepositions are used with point of time.
   Æ We shall return before/after evening/ 5 0' clock/ July.
   Æ He was born before/after 1985.
   Æ She has been studying since morning.
   Æ We waited till/until evening/7:30. (We did not wait after that time.)
Note: ‘Till’ is more informal than ‘until’. In the beginning of a sentence 'until' is used.
                                             Use of between, by, up to
‘Between’ is used to denote a starting point of time and a ﬁnishing point of time. ‘By’ is used to denote a
point of time, ‘up to’ is also used to denote a point of time.
   Æ The shop remains closed between 2 pm and 4pm.
   Æ He will ﬁnish his work up to next week.
   Æ They had completed their homework by 6 o'clock.
                             Use of through, throughout, towards, behind, to, past
   Æ The day became cold towards evening. (preposition of time)
   Æ The army headed towards the border. (preposition of place)
   Æ Hurry up! We are behind time/schedule. (late)
   Æ What is the time? It is ten minutes to two. It is ten minutes past two.
   Æ It was past midnight when we reached home.

   Æ   You should ﬁght for your rights.
   Æ   He died of cancer.
   Æ   She is suffering from typhoid.
   Æ   She failed due to her carelessness.
                                    Prepositions of Direction
Prepositions of direction denote direction or movement of persons or things with respect to someone or
something else. Examples: to, towards, into, at, for, against, off, around, along, across, up, down etc.
