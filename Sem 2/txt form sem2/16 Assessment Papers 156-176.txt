Ships are large vessels that ﬂoat on water. These vehicles are different from boats in view of their sizes and
passenger capacity. Ships are engaged for a variety of activities such as transporting people or goods, ﬁshing and
warfare.
Passenger ships include ferries which are used for short-sea trips, in moving passengers and vehicles. Ocean
liners are typical passenger or passenger cargo vessels transporting only passengers. We can also see cruise ships
at sea. These ships often transport passengers on round trips in which the trip and the attractions on the ship are a
major draw.
Luxury cruise ships are used for pleasure voyages. They are almost like moving seven star hotels on the sea,
offering professional hospitality. These ships have big restaurants, theatres, pools, gymnasiums, beauty parlours,
saloons, and duty-free shops. Passenger ships or cruises have single or shared cabins, deluxe rooms, penthouses,
rooms and dormitories.
Some ships carry cargo all over the world. The ships that carry bulk cargo are called cargo ships. Even these ships
travel all over the world-east, west, north, and south. They carry food items such as wheat, rice, meat, and
oranges. Some ships carry bulk liquids in the form of fuel or oil. These are also called oil tankers. Others also
carry raw materials like coal and iron ore. Some ships carry industrial products such as cars, televisions, and
computers. Cargo is stored in the inside of a ship called the ‘holds’.
Ships on the ocean and sea also maintain the trafﬁc rules like vehicles on land. They cannot travel everywhere in
narrow straits and at the entrance to harbours. They are also designated sea routes for ships. Near the shore there
are shallow places, and a big ship can run aground and damage its bottom or sides. Ships that enter port must do so
only during designated times, that is, at high tide when there is a current which draws the water into the harbour.

Dolls are among the oldest creations of man. Archaeologists have found small dolls of clay, wood, bone and ivory
from graves and other areas excavated. In India, the earliest dolls, unearthed from the site of the Indus valley
civilization, are as old as ﬁve thousand years. The earliest dolls were made from natural objects like sticks and
stones. American Indians made a deity of wood, and used it while imparting religious instructions.
Dolls of cloth, wax and papier mache also became popular. Pottery dolls became common with the children of
Victorian England. They had perfect, fragile porcelain heads and rich royal clothing; Children also had a ‘Sunday
doll’, which was taken out of its glass case only on Sundays. The doll houses were elaborately set up with
furniture, pictures, decoration pieces; window drapes-almost everything required in a luxurious home.
In Korea, a traditional doll was made out of a bamboo pipe, and long grass was used to form the ‘hair’. This doll
was not given any feature on the face, but some white powder was rubbed over a small area. Some of the earliest
Russian dolls were made of pine cones and these were clothed with moss cloaks. Another Russian doll is the
‘matryoshka’ doll, also known as a Russian nesting doll of decreasing sizes placed one inside the other.
The Japanese used dolls for various purposes. A primitive Japanese doll was made of sticks and strings, covered
with paper clothing. Some dolls were ‘dressed’ and ‘fed’ and ‘treated’ as though alive. The Japanese also have
ceremonial dolls, not for playing but for simply exhibiting on a speciﬁc day. The boys exhibit their warrior dolls
on March 3, and the girls’ festival falls on May 5.
The use of dolls in religious ceremonies continues till date. Scenes of the birth of Jesus include ﬁgures of baby
Jesus, Mother Mary, Joseph and the shepherds. These are called crib dolls and are not meant for playing. In
northern India, the story of Krishna’s birth is depicted through clay dolls. In Bihar, the entire story of the Shyama
Chak festival is depicted through clay images.
Even today, craftsmen in Assam and West Bengal make dolls out of soft stems of a plant growing in marshy areas.
West Bengal is known for terracotta toys, and Rajasthan markets dolls of unbaked clay. Artists of Uttar Pradesh
are masters of making brightly painted wooden dolls. Over the years, the variety of materials and mechanisms
used for making dolls has increased tremendously.
