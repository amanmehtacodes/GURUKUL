Ships are large vessels that float on water. These vehicles are different from boats in view of their sizes and passenger capacity. Ships are engaged for a variety of activities such as transporting people or goods, fishing and warfare.

Passenger ships include ferries which are used for short-sea trips, in moving passengers and vehicles. Ocean liners are typical passenger or passenger cargo vessels transporting only passengers. We can also see cruise ships at sea. These ships often transport passengers on round trips in which the trip and the attractions on the ship are a major draw.

Luxury cruise ships are used for pleasure voyages. They are almost like moving seven star hotels on the sea, offering professional hospitality. These ships have big restaurants, theatres, pools, gymnasiums, beauty parlours, saloons, and duty-free shops. Passenger ships or cruises have single or shared cabins, deluxe rooms, penthouses, rooms and dormitories.

Some ships carry cargo all over the world. The ships that carry bulk cargo are called cargo ships. Even these ships travel all over the world — east, west, north, and south. Others also carry raw materials like coal and iron ore. Some ships carry industrial products such as cars, televisions, and computers. Cargo is stored in the inside of a ship called the 'holds'.

Ships on the ocean and sea also maintain the traffic rules like vehicles on land. They cannot travel everywhere in narrow straits and at the entrance to harbours. They are also designated sea routes for ships. Near the shore there are shallow places, and a big ship can run aground and damage its bottom or sides. 6. Write an article on the "Effects of Heavy Bags on Children" mentioning reasons and effects, and suggesting some ways to reduce the load of the bags. (6 marks)

Sample Answer

Effects of Heavy Bags on Children
By Aman Mehta

Most schoolchildren today carry bags that weigh far more than what is recommended for their age. This happens because of overloaded timetables, extra textbooks, and unnecessary items carried "just in case." Carrying such heavy bags every day can cause back pain, poor posture, and even long-term spinal problems in growing children.
Schools can help by reducing the number of textbooks needed each day, encouraging the use of lockers, and prescribing lighter bag materials. Parents, too, should check their child's bag daily and remove anything not needed for that day. With these small steps, we can protect children's health without compromising their education.

5.Quick Revision Checklist

  • Reading: always answer comprehension questions in your own words where possible, and lift the exact word from the poem or passage only when the question explicitly asks for a "word that means the same as."
  • Writing: identify the format first (notice, diary, letter, message, application, article, speech, e-mail, biographical sketch, story) and follow its layout exactly — marks are given for format as well as content.
  • Editing (error correction): read line by line; common errors are wrong verb agreement, wrong preposition, and wrong article — check subject-verb agreement first.
  • Editing (omission): read each line aloud — the missing word is usually a preposition, article, or auxiliary verb that would make the sentence flow naturally.
  • Cloze passages: read the whole sentence (not just around the blank) before choosing an option — tense and subject often depend on words later in the sentence.
  • Voice change and narration: identify the tense of the original sentence first, then apply the standard shift rules — these MCQs test whether you know the rule, not just the vocabulary.
  • Always check the marks allotted next to each question — it tells you roughly how many points/sentences your answer should cover.
