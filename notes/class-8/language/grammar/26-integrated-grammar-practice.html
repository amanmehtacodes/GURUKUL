2.News Headlines — Matching Exercise

There is no denying the fact that reading newspaper is a tried and tested way of improving one's language. A newspaper has various kinds of news items covering areas such as politics, sports, business, entertainment, advertisements, notices, articles, letters to the editor etc and hence is a very rich source of vocabulary. The language used in news headlines is made compact by using non-finite verbs, phrases and passive structures in order to provide maximum information using minimum number of words in an impressive way. The news headlines are often catchy and spicy to attract the readers' attention.

3.Interpreting and Explaining News Headlines

Since news headlines are written in pithy and compressed language they often employ tense or verb forms different from the ones used in the explanation. Therefore, it is important to identify the verb form or the tense used in the headline, understand its correct meaning and explain the news item using appropriate tense. Read the table given below to understand the use of different tenses and verb forms for news headlines and their explanation.

News HeadlineTense/Verb Form UsedExplanation
8 Killed, 10 hurt in pile-up due to dense fog off JhajjarPast Participle used to denote a past accident: killed, hurtEight people, including seven women, were killed and 10 injured in a massive pile-up of dozens of vehicles, including school buses, near Badli bypass on Jhajjar-Rewari highway on Monday morning due to low visibility caused by heavy fog.
65 US varsities move court on visa changesSimple Present used to denote a recent action: MoveAs many as 65 top US universities, including Harvard and MIT, have challenged the Trump administration in a court against its new stringent visa policy for foreign students, warning that the new 'backdating' rule will have a detrimental effect on America's higher education system.
PM likely to flag off first electric engine converted from diesel locos at DLWInfinitive with to used to denote a future action: to flag offPrime Minister Narendra Modi is likely to flag-off the world's first electric engine converted from diesel locomotive during his visit to the city on December 29.
GST having negative effect on tourism in J&K: Par panelPresent Participle used to denote an existing situation: havingThe Goods and Services Tax has had a negative effect on tourism in Jammu and Kashmir, a parliamentary panel has noted, adding the Centre should reconsider the indirect tax regime on tourism-related activities in the northern state.

Points to Remember

  1. Use of Simple Present Tense: Simple Present Tense is excessively used in news headlines. In news headlines, Simple Present Tense is used to denote an action that somebody has done in recent past or is likely to do in near future. The reporting of a news headline in the present tense can be done in simple present, simple past or simple future depending on the news. For a report in simple past, past time reference is important.
  2. Use of Infinitive with to: In news headlines, Infinitive with 'to' is used to denote an action that someone is going to do in near future.
  3. Use of Past Participle: In news headlines, Past Participle is used to denote a verb in passive form to suggest that something has been done to some subject.
  4. Use of Present Participle: In news headlines, Present Participle can be used to denote an action of present continuous tense or a future action.
  5. Use of Simple Past Tense: While reporting a news headline, if past time is added, it is written in simple past tense.
  6. Voice: The report of a news headline may be written in active or passive voice depending on the sentence structures used in the report.

11.Quick Revision Checklist

  • Sentence reordering rewards finding the subject first, then building outward — verb next, then objects and modifiers last.
  • News headlines compress grammar for space: infinitives (to flag off) signal the future, past participles (killed, hurt) signal the past passively, and present participles (having) signal an ongoing situation.
  • Explaining a headline means expanding its compressed grammar back into a full sentence with a proper subject, tense, and connecting words.
  • Cloze passages with multiple-choice options are solved by testing each option in the sentence and checking subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, and the right preposition.
  • In error-correction editing, each flagged line has exactly one wrong word — check spelling, tense, number agreement, and word choice (its/it's, than/then) in that order.
  • In omission editing, read across the line boundary — the missing word is often a small connector (a, the, to, that, was) sitting right at the seam between two lines.
  • Describing a process in the passive voice turns each imperative instruction into Subject + is/are/was/were/will be + past participle, keeping the same sequence as the original steps.
