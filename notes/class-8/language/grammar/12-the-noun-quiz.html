Quiz 12 — The Noun

A fresh set of noun exercises — a follow-up to Lesson 12, not a repeat of it.

How this works: unlike the writing-skills quizzes, noun exercises have definite correct answers. Fill in your attempt in each box, then press the reveal button under each exercise to check your answer against the key.

1.Kinds of Nouns

Identify whether each underlined noun is Common (C), Proper (P), Collective (Co), Abstract (A) or Material (M).

  1. The jury gave its verdict.
  2. My courage did not fail me.
  3. The ring is made of silver.
  4. Kolkata is a busy city.
  5. The teacher praised the class.
Answer Key

1. Collective · 2. Abstract · 3. Material · 4. Proper · 5. Common

2.Collective Nouns

Fill in the blanks with the correct collective nouns.

  1. a of grapes
  2. a of geese
  3. a of jurors
  4. a of wolves
  5. a of grass
Answer Key

1. bunch/cluster of grapes · 2. gaggle/flock of geese · 3. panel of jurors · 4. pack of wolves · 5. patch/blade of grass

3.Forming Abstract Nouns

Form abstract nouns from the words in brackets.

  1. His impressed everyone at the interview. (confident)
  2. The of the crowd was overwhelming. (excited)
  3. We admired her in the crisis. (brave)
  4. His cost him the match. (arrogant)
  5. Her towards animals is well known. (kind)
Answer Key

1. confidence · 2. excitement · 3. bravery · 4. arrogance · 5. kindness

6.Noun Case — Identify the Function

Underline the nouns in each sentence and state their function (subject, direct object, indirect object, object of preposition, subject complement, object complement, nominative of address, or apposition).

  1. Neha, my cousin, won the debate competition.
  2. The manager gave the intern a difficult task.
  3. Boys, listen to the announcement carefully.
  4. My brother is an engineer.
Answer Key

1. Neha — subject; cousin — noun in apposition; competition — direct object.
2. manager — subject; intern — indirect object; task — direct object.
3. Boys — nominative of address; announcement — direct object.
4. brother — subject; engineer — subject complement.

7.The Possessive Apostrophe

Correct the possessive case in each phrase.

  1. The ladys' bag
  2. Charles's sake
  3. The table's surface
  4. My sister's-in-law car
  5. Ramesh and Suresh's shop (they own separate shops)
Answer Key

1. The lady's bag · 2. Charles' sake (hissing sound) · 3. the surface of the table (inanimate) · 4. My sister-in-law's car (compound noun) · 5. Ramesh's and Suresh's shop (separate possession)
