1.Warm-up — What Adjectives Do

Read the following sentences and observe what the words in bold blue tell about a noun or a pronoun.

  1. Ram is an intelligent boy. (describes the quality of boy)
  2. She is beautiful. (describes the quality 'she')
  3. This is my new car. (describes the state of the car)
  4. This is a nice book. (describes opinion about the book)
  5. I have a rectangular table. (describes shape of the table)
  6. I saw a black horse. (describes colour of the horse)
  7. They have bought a big house. (describes size of the house)
  8. Robert is an American. (describes nationality)
  9. My father is old. (describes age of father)
  10. I have ten pens. (describes number of pens)
  11. Give me some rice. (describes quantity of rice)

You can observe that the words in bold blue font in the above sentences describe the quality, opinion, state, age, colour, shape, size, number, quantity, material or nationality of the nouns or the pronouns in the given sentences. These words that are used for people or things are called adjectives.

Definition: An adjective is a word that describes or explains a noun or a pronoun.

4.Adjectives of Quality

Adjectives of quality describe the kind, quality or characteristics of a noun or a pronoun. These adjectives describe size, colour, shape, state, age, opinion, material etc. of a noun or a pronoun.

Examples: Red bangles, circular motion, huge ship, old memories, interesting story, common name, silver lining, etc.

Proper Adjectives: Adjectives which are formed from proper nouns are called proper adjectives. Examples: Indian Flag, Chinese toys, British Airways, American author etc.

5.Adjectives of Number

An adjective of number, as the name suggests, describes the number of a noun or a pronoun. It answers the question, 'how many'. It is used for countable nouns or pronouns. There are two types of adjectives of number.

  1. Definite Numeral Adjectives: indicate the exact number of a noun or a pronoun. Example: Two boys are playing in the park. There are two types of definite numeral adjectives.
    • Cardinals: denote the exact number of a noun or a pronoun. Example: There are five boys in this row.
    • Ordinals: denote the order or rank of a noun or a pronoun. Examples: first, second, third, tenth, last, next etc. Article 'the' or a possessive determiner is used before an ordinal. Example: The fifth boy in this row is my friend.
  2. Indefinite Numeral Adjectives: don't indicate the exact number of the noun or pronoun they refer to. Examples: Several people are waiting in the queue. There are many birds on this tree.

6.Adjectives of Quantity

An adjective of quantity indicates the quantity of a noun or a pronoun. It answers the question, 'how much'. It is used for uncountable nouns or pronouns.

Examples: some, any, no, much, little, half, all, whole, sufficient, enough, great, etc.

1. I ate some rice. 2. There is not much oil in the bottle. 3. I don't have any money. 4. This statement makes no sense. 5. There is sufficient milk in the jug. 6. I don't have enough time to waste on these matters.

7.Possessive Adjectives

The words 'my', 'our', 'your', 'his', 'her', 'its' and 'their' are used as possessive adjectives.

Examples: 1. India is our country. 2. You must complete your work. 3. It is her notebook. 4. The dog wagged its tail.

Possessive Pronouns and Possessive Adjectives

Both possessive pronouns and possessive adjectives are used to show possession or belonging. However, whereas pronouns replace the nouns, adjectives describe the nouns. Possessive adjectives are placed before nouns.

Possessive PronounPossessive Adjective
This book is mine.This is my book.
That house is yours.That is your house.
These bags are theirs.These are their bags.
This pencil is hers.This is her pencil.

Important Note: Don't use any apostrophe ('s) with possessive adjectives. The only exception is the pronoun 'one' which takes an apostrophe ('s) to make its possessive adjective. Example: One must do one's duty.

8.Demonstrative Adjectives

Definition: The words 'this', 'that', 'these', 'those' and 'such' are called demonstratives as they are used to point out to persons or things they refer to.

  1. This pencil is mine. (Used for singular thing which is near the speaker)
  2. That car is new. (Used for singular thing which is at a distance from the speaker)
  3. These kites are mine. (Used for plural things which are near the speaker)
  4. Those oranges are fresh. (Used for plural things which are at a distance from the speaker)
  5. I don't like such people.

Demonstrative Pronouns and Demonstrative Adjectives

Both demonstrative pronouns and demonstrative adjectives are used to point out a noun or a pronoun they refer to. However, whereas pronouns replace the nouns, adjectives describe the nouns.

Demonstrative PronounDemonstrative Adjective

This is her pencil.This pencil is hers.

9.Interrogative Adjectives

The words 'what', 'which' and 'whose' are interrogative pronouns used to ask questions about persons or things. These words can be used as interrogative adjectives too. When used just before a noun, these words become interrogative adjectives.

Interrogative PronounInterrogative Adjective
Which of these is your book?Which book is yours?
What is the news he broke to you?What news did he break to you?
Whose is this house?Whose house is this?

10.Distributive Adjectives

Definition: The adjectives that refer to a single person or thing in a group, one at a time, are called distributive adjectives. 'each', 'every', 'either', 'neither' are called distributive adjectives. Nouns preceded by distributive adjectives always take a singular verb.

Each and Every

'Each' and 'every' are similar in meaning, but there is a difference between the two. Each is used when speaking of two or more persons or things. 'Every' is used when speaking of more than two persons or things. Each directs attention to every person or thing of a group individually and separately. 'Every' is comprehensive, considering all; it directs attention to the whole group without any exception. Each is used to denote a person or thing out of a known or limited group. 'Every' is used in a wide sense to denote persons or things when the number is unlimited.

Examples: 1. Each child was given a sweet. (considering each individual out of a limited group) 2. Every child likes sweets. (considering all in an unlimited group)

Either and Neither

'Either' has two meanings: (i) One out of two (ii) Each of two. 'Neither' means none out of two.

Examples: 1. There are trees on either side of the road. (each out of two) 2. Neither side of the road has trees. (none out of two)

Distributive Pronouns and Distributive Adjectives

Distributive PronounDistributive Adjective

Either of the two boys had completed his work.Either boy completed his work.
Neither of the sides of road has trees.Neither side of the road has trees.

11.Emphasizing & Exclamatory Adjectives

Emphasizing Adjectives

Emphasizing adjectives lay stress on a particular person or thing.

Examples: 1. His very name makes me smile. 2. He is making a pot with his own hands.

Exclamatory Adjectives

Exclamatory adjectives are used before nouns in an exclamatory sentence.

Examples: 1. What a beautiful house! 2. What good luck!

12.Formation of Adjectives

  • Some words are independent adjectives. They are not formed from any other part of speech. Examples: rich, hot, yellow, thin etc.
  • Some adjectives are formed from verbs by adding the suffix '-ing'. These adjectives are called present participle. Examples: a moving train, a dancing doll, a burning house etc.
  • Sometimes the third form of the verb is used as adjective. This is called past participle. Examples: a locked house, a broken ship, a chained elephant etc.
  • Most adjectives are formed from nouns, verbs and adjectives by adding suffixes. A few examples are given below.

13.Degrees of Adjectives

There are three degrees of adjectives.

  1. Positive Degree: used when the adjective describes only one thing or person without making any comparison. Even if there is any comparison involved, it is between one and none. Examples: Ram is a tall boy. (Describing only Ram) No boy in the class is as tall as Ram. (Comparison between one and none)
  2. Comparative Degree: used when the adjective compares two persons, things or groups. Here, the mode of comparison is 'one to one'. The comparative degree is followed by the conjunction 'than'. The comparison may be made in three ways:
    • Individual compared with individual — Ram is taller than Shyam.
    • Individual compared with a group — Ram is taller than any other boy in the class.
    • Group compared with a group — The players of team A are taller than the players of team B.
  3. Superlative Degree: used when one person or thing is compared with all others in the group. Here the mode of comparison is 'one to all'. Example: Ram is the tallest boy in the class.

15.Verbs Used as Adjectives

The present participle form and the past participle form of the verb can also be used as an adjective. These are called verbal adjectives.

Examples: 1. I have a dancing doll. 2. The movie was interesting. 3. We found the house locked. 4. The lost child was crying bitterly.

In the examples given above, the words 'dancing' and 'interesting' are the present participle forms of the verbs describing the nouns 'doll' and 'movie' respectively. Similarly, the words 'locked' and 'lost' are the past participle forms of the verbs describing the nouns 'house' and 'child' respectively.

16.Use of Adjectives in Similes

A simile is a figure of speech often employed in poetry to make comparisons between persons, animals or objects. Adjectives are frequently used in similes.

Examples: as busy as bee, as soft as silk, as easy as A.B.C.

17.Transformation of Sentences by Using Degrees

Read the following three sentences carefully and observe the way of transformation.

1. No boy in the class is as intelligent as Ram. (Positive) 2. Ram is more intelligent than any other boy in the class. (Comparative) 3. Ram is the most intelligent boy in the class. (Superlative)

The meaning of all three sentences is the same — Ram is the most intelligent boy, the only one who is. However, this sense is conveyed by using the Positive, Comparative and Superlative Degrees of the Adjective.

1. Few boys in the class are as intelligent as Ram. 2. Ram is more intelligent than most other boys in the class. 3. Ram is one of the most intelligent boys in the class.

Here, Ram is one of the most intelligent boys in the class — he is not the only one.

1. Ram is not the tallest of all the boys in the class. 2. Ram is not taller than some other boys in the class. 3. Some other boys in the class are at least as tall as Ram.

Ritika is not as beautiful as Rashmi. This sentence compares two people. It can be rewritten without changing the sense: Rashmi is more beautiful than Ritika.

Abdul is as strong as his brother. This too compares two people. It can be rewritten: Abdul's brother is not stronger than he is.
