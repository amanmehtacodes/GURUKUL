1.What Is Narration?

Read the sentences given below.

My father said to me, "I have to attend a meeting tomorrow."
My father told me that he had to attend a meeting the next day.

The act of reporting a person's speech is called Narration. There are two ways of narrating a person's speech.

Direct Speech: In Direct Speech, the actual words spoken by a person are quoted within inverted commas ("..........."). In example 1, the actual words spoken by 'my father' are quoted without any change.

Indirect Speech: In Indirect Speech, the gist/meaning/matter of the speaker's words is conveyed without quoting the actual words. In example 2, the meaning of "my father's" words is conveyed without quoting his actual words.

Two Parts of a Direct Speech

  • (i) Reporting Speech: The part of the sentence outside the inverted commas is called Reporting Speech. It is generally termed as Reporting Verb. It has three parts: Subject, Reporting Verb and Object. In certain cases the object may be missing.
  • (ii) Reported Speech: The part of the sentence enclosed within the inverted commas is called the Reported Speech. A Reported Speech also has a Subject and a Verb with other extensions (other parts of speech).

Read the sentences given below carefully and study the structures of a Direct Sentence.

  1. My friend said to me, "I will give you my book."
  2. (Reporting Speech), "Reported Speech."
  3. (My friend) (said) (to me), "I will give you my book."
  4. (Subject) (Reporting Verb) (Object), "Subject and Verb of Reported Speech (with other extensions)."

Sentence Structure of an Indirect Speech

An Indirect Speech is a Complex Sentence made of a Principal Clause (Reporting Speech) and a Subordinate Clause (Reported Speech), generally joined by a conjunction. Look at the following example:

Direct: My friend said to me, "I will give you my book."
Indirect: My friend told me that he would give me his book.

2.General Rules I & II — Inverted Commas, Tense of Reporting Verb

Important Note: General rules are those rules which are always followed while changing a Direct Speech into an Indirect Speech. Both general and specific Rules must be followed while changing a Direct Speech into an Indirect Speech. The specific rules are related to types of sentences. These rules are discussed further in this chapter.

Rule-I Inverted commas are removed.

Rule-II Look at the tense of the reporting verb.

  1. (a) If the tense of Reporting Verb in Direct Speech is Present or Future Tense, there will be no change in the tense of the Reported Speech while changing the Direct Speech into Indirect Speech.
    Direct: The teacher says to the students, "I have corrected your notebooks." (Present Perfect Tense)
    Indirect: The teacher tells the students that she has corrected their notebooks. (Present Perfect Tense)
  2. (b) If the tense of Reporting Verb in Direct Speech is Past Tense, follow the rules regarding the change of tense discussed later in this chapter.

3.General Rule III — Personal Pronouns

Important Note: While changing the Personal Pronouns, the Gender and Case of the Personal Pronouns should be kept the same. Study the table given below.

Personal PronounsNominative CaseAccusative CasePossessive Case
First PersonI, weme, usmy, mine, our, ours
Second Personyou, thouyou, theeyour, yours, thine
Third Personhe, she, it, theyhim, her, it, themhis, her, hers, its, their, theirs
  1. (i) Pronouns of the First Person in the Reported Speech change according to the Subject of the Reporting Verb. The case will remain the same.
    Direct: My sister said to me, "I don't want to waste my time." — My sister (Subject, Third Person) said to me, "I (First person, Nominative Case) don't want to waste my (First Person, Possessive Case) time."
    Indirect: My sister told me that she (Third person, Nominative Case) did not want to waste her (Third Person, Possessive Case) time.
  2. (ii) Pronouns of the Second Person in the Reported Speech change according to the Object of the Reporting Verb. The case will remain the same.
    Direct: I said to her, "You should do your work." — I said to her (Object, Third Person), "You (Second person, Nominative Case) should do your (Second Person, Possessive Case) work."
    Indirect: I advised her that she (Third Person, Nominative Case) should do her (Third Person, Possessive Case) work.
  3. (iii) Pronouns of the Third Person in the Reported Speech remain unchanged.
    Direct: Asha said to me, "He (Third Person) is wasting his (Third Person) time."
    Indirect: Asha told me that he (Third Person) was wasting his (Third Person) time.

4.General Rules IV–VII — Agreement, Conjunctions, Say→Tell, Vocatives

Rule-IV Subject-verb agreement must be followed when Pronouns or Tense in the Reported Speech is changed.

Direct: I say to my friend, "You are very intelligent." → Indirect: I tell my friend that he is very intelligent.
Direct: I said to my friend, "You are very intelligent." → Indirect: I told my friend that he was very intelligent.

Rule-VI The tense of Reporting Verb remains the same. If the Reporting Verb is 'say', 'says', 'will say', or 'said' it remains the same. If Reporting Verb is followed by 'to' and an object, there will be the following changes in the Reporting Verbs if the Reported Speech is an Assertive Sentence. Examples:

Rule-VII Vocatives and Nominative of Address are omitted altogether and their sense expressed in the sentence.

Direct: He said to his boss, "Sir, the report is ready."
Indirect: He told his boss respectfully that the report was ready.

5.Rules Regarding the Change in Tense — Rules I & II

Rule-I If the Reporting Verb is in Present Tense or Future Tense, there will be no change in the tense of the Reported Speech.

Direct: My mother says, "The cake is delicious." → Indirect: My mother says that the cake is delicious.
Direct: He says to me, "I am playing here." → Indirect: He tells me that he is playing here.
Direct: Tarun will say, "I was reading a book." → Indirect: Tarun will say that he was reading a book.

Rule-II If the Reporting Verb is in Past Tense the tense of the Reported Speech will change to its corresponding tense in the Past Tense. The following table explains the change in tense.

Rule-IV — Change of 'Must':

  1. (i) If 'must' indicates future necessity or compulsion, it changes into would+have+to+V1 (Conditional).
    Direct: He said, "I must finish this work by tomorrow."
    Indirect: He said that he would have to finish that work by the next day.
  2. (ii) For immediate necessity, 'must' is changed into 'had to'.
    Direct: He said, "I must go home now."
    Indirect: He said that he had to go home then.
  3. (iii) When 'must' indicates permanent rule, advice, prohibition etc, it does not change.
    Direct: The teacher said, "The students must not miss my class."
    Indirect: The teacher said that the students must not miss her class.

Rule-V — Change of Modal Auxiliaries 'Need' and 'Dare':

Direct: He said to me, "You need not wait for me."
Indirect: He told me that I need not wait for him. Or, He told me that I did not need to wait for him.

Direct: He said to me, "I dare not talk to the boss."
Indirect: He told me that he dare not talk to the boss. Or, He told me that he did not dare to talk to the boss.

8.Situations When the Tense of Reported Speech Remains the Same

There are a few exceptional situations when the tense of the Reported Speech is not changed even if the Reporting Verb is in the Past Tense.

  1. (a) Universal Truth, Mathematical/Geographical/Historical/Chemical fact, or other fact.
    Direct: The teacher said, "The sun rises in the east." → Indirect: The teacher said that the sun rises in the east.
    Direct: He said to me, "Two and two make four." → Indirect: He told me that two and two make four.
    Direct: He said to me, "Water freezes at 0° Celsius." → Indirect: He told me that water freezes at 0° Celsius.
    Direct: He said to me, "The Red Fort is in Delhi." → Indirect: He told me that the Red Fort is in Delhi.
  2. (b) Proverb or an act of morality.
    Direct: Riya said to her brother, "A burnt child dreads the fire." → Indirect: Riya told her brother that a burnt child dreads the fire.
  3. (c) Habitual Act of Present.
    Direct: The teacher said, "Mohan always comes late." → Indirect: The teacher said that Mohan always comes late. (He still comes late.)
    Direct: He said to me, "My mother goes to temple every day." → Indirect: He told me that his mother goes to temple every day. (Present habit)
  4. (d) Reported Speech refers to past time/date/year.
    Direct: My mother said to me, "India got freedom on 15th August 1947." → Indirect: My mother told me that India got freedom on 15th August 1947.
  5. (e) Two simultaneous actions.
    Direct: He said to me, "I studied when my brother played." → Indirect: He told me that he studied when his brother played.
    Direct: He said to me, "I was completing my homework when my father was watching TV." → Indirect: He told me that he was completing his homework when his father was watching TV.
  6. (f) Action expressed continues at the time of Reporting.
    Direct: He said to me, "Prices are going up." → Indirect: He told me that prices are going up.
    Direct: He said to me, "India is progressing by leaps and bounds." → Indirect: He told me that India is progressing by leaps and bounds.
  7. (g) Imaginary or unreal condition.
    Direct: He said, "If my son were here, I would be very happy." → Indirect: He said that if his son were there, he would be very happy.
    Direct: He said to me, "If you had invited me, I would have attended the party." → Indirect: He told me that if I had invited him, he would have attended the party.
    Direct: He said, "It is high time we started our journey." → Indirect: He said that it was high time they started their journey.

9.Types of Sentences in Reported Speech

There are different rules for different types of sentences for changing a Direct Speech into an Indirect Speech. Therefore, it is very important to find out what type of sentence a Reported Speech is. A Reported Speech in a Direct Speech may be:

  1. Assertive Sentence
  2. Interrogative Sentence
  3. Imperative Sentence
  4. Exclamatory Sentence
  5. Optative Sentence

10.Rules/Instructions for Changing Assertive Sentences

Assertive Sentences are simple statements. There are two types: Affirmative Sentences and Negative Sentences.

  1. Remove inverted commas and use the conjunction 'that' to join the Reporting Speech and the Reported Speech. Change Reporting Verb as explained under Rule-VI of general rules.
  2. Change Personal Pronouns according to General rules.
  3. Follow Tense related rules to change or not to change the Tense.
  4. Adverbs of time and place and other words are changed as explained under Rule-VI of changing tense.

Direct: He said, "I am reading a book." → Indirect: He said that he was reading a book.
Direct: My friend said to me, "I have solved the sum." → Indirect: My friend told me that he had solved the sum.
Direct: He said to me, "I cannot explain the situation now." → Indirect: He told me that he could not explain the situation then.
Direct: She said to me, "I was waiting for you." → Indirect: She told me that she had been waiting for me.
Direct: My brother said to me, "I will work hard to win the gold medal in the race." → Indirect: My brother told me that he would work hard to win the gold medal in the race.

13.Rules/Instructions for Changing Interrogative Sentences

Interrogative Sentences are questions. There are two types of Interrogative Sentences.

(a) Single Interrogative Sentence: It starts with a Primary Auxiliary or a Modal Auxiliary. Such sentences are answered in 'Yes' or 'No'.
Examples: (i) Are you a teacher? Yes/No (ii) Will you marry me? Yes/No

(b) Double Interrogative Sentence: It starts with a 'Wh-word' (Interrogative Pronoun or Interrogative Adverb).
Examples: (i) What is your name? (ii) Who are you? (iii) Where are you going?

  1. Change the reporting verb into ask/asked, enquire/enquired, or wonder/wondered etc.
  2. Remove inverted commas. If the Reported Speech is a Single Interrogative Sentence, use the conjunction 'if' or 'whether' to join the Reporting Speech and the Reported Speech.
  3. If the Reported Speech is a Double Interrogative Sentence, use the Wh-word given in the Reported Speech as a conjunction.
  4. Follow the rules of Pronouns and Tenses.
  5. In Indirect Speech, the Interrogative Sentence given in the Reported Speech changes into an Assertive Sentence. Therefore, the places of Subject and Verb are exchanged. This is known as Inversion. Sign of Interrogation (?) is removed.
    Example: Is he your friend? (Interrogative Sentence) → He is your friend. (Assertive Sentence)
  6. Helping verbs: 'do', 'does', or 'did' are removed.

Direct: He said to me, "Do you live in Chandigarh?" → Indirect: He asked me if I lived in Chandigarh.
Direct: He said to me, "When are you going to Delhi?" → Indirect: He asked me when I was going to Delhi.
Direct: The teacher said to the students, "Who has broken the window?" → Indirect: The teacher asked the students who had broken the windows.
Direct: She said to me, "Shall I open the door?" → Indirect: She asked me if she should open the door.
Direct: My father said to me, "Have you taken my pen?" → Indirect: My father asked me if I had taken his pen.
Direct: My sister said to me, "Why are you troubling me?" → Indirect: My sister asked me why I was troubling her.

Questions Starting with Shall I/We

  1. If 'shall' indicates Future Tense or some information/speculation, it changes into 'would'.
    Direct: He said to me, "Shall we ever meet again?" → Indirect: He wondered if we would ever meet again.
  2. If 'shall' indicates seeking advice or a request, it changes into 'should'.
    Direct: Ritu said, "Shall I open the door?" → Indirect: Ritu asked if she should open the door.
  3. If 'shall' indicates an offer, it is replaced with the verb 'offer'.
    Direct: She said to me, "Shall I bring you something to eat?" → Indirect: She offered to bring me something to eat.
  4. If 'shall' indicates a suggestion, it is replaced with the verb 'suggest'.
    Direct: He said, "Shall we meet again tomorrow?" → Indirect: He suggested meeting again the next day.

Questions Starting with Will you/Would you/Could you

  1. If the questions starting with 'will you', 'would you' or 'could you' are ordinary questions, the rules of single interrogative sentences will be applied.
  2. If the questions starting with 'will you', 'would you' or 'could you' indicate an order, request or offer, the changes will be made accordingly as explained in the following examples.

Direct: He shouted, "Will you keep silence?" (order) → Indirect: He ordered me to keep silence.
Direct: She said to me, "Could you give me some sugar?" (request) → Indirect: She requested me to give her some sugar.
Direct: She said to me, "Would you like to have a drink?" (offer) → Indirect: She invited me for a drink. Or, She offered me a drink.

15.Rules/Instructions for Changing Imperative Sentences

Imperative Sentences express an order, command, request, prohibition or advice. These sentences start directly with the first form of Verb or 'Don't', followed by the first form of Verb.

  1. Change the Reporting Verb into order/ordered, request/requested, command/commanded, forbid/forbade, advise/advised, or beg/begged according to the meaning implied in the Reported Speech.
  2. The Imperative Mood is changed into Infinitive Mood.
  3. Change the first form of verb into Positive Infinitive (to+V1) and 'Don't+V1' into Negative Infinitive (not+to+V1). For Negative Infinitive, the verb 'Forbid' can also be used.

Direct: The boss said to his employee, "Show me the report." → Indirect: The boss ordered his employee to show him the report.
Direct: My father said to me, "Take care of your health." → Indirect: My father advised me to take care of my health.
Direct: My sister said to me, "Please, don't disturb me." → Indirect: My sister requested me not to disturb her.
Direct: The Commander said to the soldiers, "Don't be afraid of the enemy. Move forward." → Indirect: The Commander commanded the soldiers not to be afraid of the enemy and to move forward.
Direct: The gardener said to the boys, "Don't pluck the flowers." → Indirect: The gardener forbade the boys to pluck the flowers.
Direct: He said to me, "Beware of false friends." → Indirect: He warned me against false friends.

17.Rules/Instructions for Changing Exclamatory Sentences

Exclamatory Sentences express a sudden or a strong feeling. These sentences start with an Interjection such as 'Hurrah', 'Alas', 'Oh' etc or 'What'/'How'.

  1. The Exclamatory Sentence is changed into Assertive Sentence.
  2. The Reporting Verb is changed into exclaimed or exclaimed with (Joy, sorrow, wonder etc according to the Interjection given in the Direct Speech).
  3. Conjunction 'that' is used to join the Reporting Verb and the Reported Speech.
  4. Interjection or any other exclamatory word is removed.
  5. All the rules of Pronouns and Tenses are followed.
  6. Mark of exclamation is removed.

Points to Remember

InterjectionExpresses
Ho! Hurrah! Hoo!joy or delight
What! Oh! Good Heavens!wonder/surprise
Alas! Ah! Oh! Sorry!grief, pain or regret
Hark! Hush! Lo! Behold!attention
Pooh! Pshaw!contempt
Damn!swear
Bravo!applause/encouragement
Nonsense!anger/disappointment
Fie! Shame!shame or reproof

Direct: She said, "Alas! I have injured my hand." → Indirect: She exclaimed with sorrow that she had injured her hand.
Direct: The players said, "Hurrah! We have won the match." → Indirect: The players exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.
Direct: The captain said to the players, "Bravo! You played very well today." → Indirect: The captain applauded the players saying that they had played very well that day.
Direct: She said, "How beautiful the scenery is!" → Indirect: She exclaimed that the scenery was very beautiful.
Direct: She said, "How foolish I have been!" → Indirect: She confessed that she had been very foolish.

19.Rules/Instructions for Changing Optative Sentences

Optative Sentences express a wish, desire, blessing, prayer or curse. The use of Modal Auxiliaries 'May' and 'Would' is common in these sentences. Conditional sentences using 'were' are also used to express a wish.

  1. Reporting verb is changed into wished/prayed/desired/cursed etc.
  2. The word 'wish' used in the Direct Speech is removed.
  3. If the Reporting Verb is in Past Tense, 'May' is changed into 'Might'.
  4. 'Were' used in Conditional Sentences is changed into 'had been' if the Reporting Verb is in Past Tense.
  5. We wish good morning, good afternoon, good evening or good night.
  6. We bid welcome, farewell or good-bye.

Direct: He said to me, "May you get success in life." → Indirect: He wished that I might get success in life.
Direct: He said, "Good morning, teacher." → Indirect: He wished good morning to his teacher.
Direct: She said, "Good bye, friends." → Indirect: She bade farewell to her friends.
Direct: He said, "O for a glass of water!" → Indirect: He desired strongly for a glass of water.
Direct: He said, "May God save the king." → Indirect: He prayed that God might save the king.

22.Quick Revision Checklist

  • Direct Speech quotes the exact words in inverted commas; Indirect Speech reports the meaning as one complex sentence.
  • If the reporting verb is Present or Future tense, the reported speech's tense does not change at all.
  • If the reporting verb is Past tense, shift the reported speech one step further into the past (Present→Past, Past→Past Perfect, and so on) — unless an exception applies.
  • First-person pronouns follow the subject of the reporting verb; second-person pronouns follow its object; third-person pronouns never change.
  • The conjunction depends on sentence type: 'that' for assertive/exclamatory/optative, 'if/whether' for yes-no questions, the wh-word itself for wh-questions, and 'to' for imperatives.
  • Tense stays the same for universal truths, proverbs, habitual acts, historical dates, simultaneous actions, ongoing situations, and unreal conditions.
  • Adverbs of time and place shift too — but only when the tense itself shifts: this→that, here→there, now→then, today→that day, tomorrow→the next day.
  • Imperatives need a reporting verb that matches the intent — order, request, advise, forbid, beg — plus an infinitive (to/not to) instead of the base verb.
  • Exclamatory and optative sentences both become assertive statements joined by 'that', with the reporting verb carrying the original emotion (exclaimed with joy, wished, prayed).
