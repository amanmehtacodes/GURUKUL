1.What Is Active and Passive Voice?

Read the following pair of sentences carefully.

  1. He reads a book.
  2. A book is read by him.

The meaning of both sentences is the same. However, the structure is different. In Sentence 1, the subject of the verb 'reads' is 'He'. Here, the subject 'he' is the active doer of the verb/action. Hence, the sentence is in active voice.

In sentence 2, the subject of the verb 'is read' is 'A book'. Here, the subject is not the active doer of the verb/action. It is the passive receiver of the action. Hence, the sentence is in passive voice.

Active Voice: When the verb shows that the subject does something, it is said to be in Active Voice.
Passive Voice: When the verb shows that something is done to the subject, it is said to be in Passive Voice.

Now read the following examples carefully and observe how sentences are changed from active to passive voice.

Active VoicePassive Voice
Boys are playing cricket.Cricket is being played by boys.
Children made a noise.A noise was made by children.
She teaches us maths.We are taught maths by her.
This box contains sweets.Sweets are contained in this box.
He knows me.I am known to him.

2.Steps and Rules for Changing Voice

The following steps are followed while converting a sentence from active to passive voice.

  1. The object of the active verb is put in the place of subject.
  2. The active verb is changed into passive form by using a form of 'be' according to the tense of the active voice and the III form of the verb. Other auxiliaries are also used according to the tense.
  3. The passive verb should be in agreement with the object which is brought forward.
  4. The subject is kept after the passive verb and it is preceded by the preposition 'by', thus converting the subject into agent or doer.
  5. In most of the cases preposition 'by' is used before agent. In certain cases preposition other than 'by' is used.

Important Rules to Follow While Changing the Active Voice into Passive Voice

  1. Don't change the tense.
  2. The passive voice of future continuous tense and all perfect continuous tenses is not possible.
  3. Don't change the type of sentence.
  4. Subject-verb agreement must be followed in passive voice too.

  5. By whom is the door being knocked at?
  6. The deserted house was peeped into by the students.
  7. The project has been worked on by them for two months.
  8. India was ruled over by the British for two hundred years.
  9. More than sixty people were run over by a speeding train.
  10. Was the meeting presided over by the CEO?

16.Transitive Verbs with Double Objects

Transitive verbs with double objects can be changed into passive voice by changing either object into subject. However, it is preferable to make the indirect object the subject of the passive verb.

Active VoicePassive Voice (Direct Object)Passive Voice (Indirect Object — preferred)
She teaches us maths.Maths is taught to us by her.We are taught maths by her.
My father gifted me a watch.A watch was gifted to me by my father.I was gifted a watch by my father.

17.Passive Voice of Sentences with Subject Understood

Sometimes the doer of the action is too obvious or unimportant to state, so the 'by' agent is dropped altogether.

Active VoicePassive Voice
The teacher has corrected my notebook.My notebook has been corrected.
The police have arrested the thief.The thief has been arrested.
Source noteThe textbook prints these two example rows with the Active/Passive labels swapped for the second row ("Passive Voice: The police have arrested the thief." followed by "Active Voice: The thief has been arrested."). The labels are corrected here to match the actual voice of each sentence, since "The police have arrested the thief" is clearly the active form and "The thief has been arrested" is clearly the passive form.

18.Quick Revision Checklist

  • Active voice: the subject does the action. Passive voice: something is done to the subject.
  • To convert: object becomes subject, verb becomes a form of 'be' + V3, and the old subject follows 'by' as the agent.
  • Never change the tense or the sentence type (statement/question/command) when converting voice.
  • Future continuous and all perfect continuous tenses cannot be turned into passive voice.
  • Prepositional verbs keep their preposition in the passive: 'looked after', 'shouted at', 'ruled over'.
  • Sentences with two objects prefer the indirect object as the passive subject: 'We are taught maths by her', not 'Maths is taught to us by her'.
  • Imperative sentences become 'You are requested/advised/ordered to...' or 'Let... be...' in the passive.
  • When the doer is obvious or unimportant, the 'by' agent can be dropped entirely.
