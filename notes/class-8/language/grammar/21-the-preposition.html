1.What Is a Preposition?

A preposition (pre-position), as the name suggests, is a word which is placed before a noun or a pronoun to establish the relation between the noun or pronoun and some other word in a sentence. Thus, a preposition is always followed by a noun or a pronoun.

2.Types of Preposition

  1. Simple Preposition: in, of, on, up, with, for, at, out, off, by etc.
  2. Compound Prepositions: without, within, outside, inside, into, etc.
  3. Double Prepositions: Outside of, out of, from out, from behind, from beneath etc.
  4. Participle Prepositions: Notwithstanding, concerning, regarding, considering, etc.
  5. Phrase Prepositions: By means of, by virtue of, on account of, in opposition to, with regard to, for the sake of, instead of, on behalf of etc.

3.Prepositions of Place or Direction

Look at the pictures given below carefully to understand how prepositions can be used to indicate the relation of place or direction.

1. The place may be seen as a point or place that can be identified.

  • He went to market.
  • They waited at the main gate.
  • I remained away from home for many years.

2. The place may be seen as a line.

  • The train moved on the track.
  • The ship was anchored a mile off the coast.
  • He went across the road.
  • They walked up/down the lane.
  • They walked along the sea shore.

3. The place may be seen as a surface.

  • They sat on the desk.
  • They sat at the first desk.
  • They went across the field.
  • The apple fell on (to) the ground.

4. The place may be seen as an area which has boundaries and limits.

  • The farmer is in the field.
  • She went through the forest.
  • The thief ran into the street.
  • We stayed out of the country.
  • The children are playing in the park.

5. The place may be seen as a volume.

  • He went into the room.
  • Keep these books in the box.
  • They went out of the house.
  • The breeze blew through the trees.

6. Prepositions of Relative Position:

(a) Above, over, on top of, below, under, underneath and beneath show the relative position of two objects vertically.
(b) Before, in front of, behind, by, beside and after show the relative position of two objects horizontally.

  • The boy stood under the tree.
  • He kept the letter under the pillow. (not below the pillow)
  • I feel as if I were on top of the world.
  • There is a temple in front of my house.
  • The little child hid behind a tree.
  • There is a bridge above/over the river.
  • A river flows below the bridge.

5.Prepositions of Time — Use of At

Time has two dimensions: Point of time and Period of time.

  • For point of time, chiefly clock time: at 6 o'clock, at noon, at night, at midnight
  • For holiday periods: at Christmas, at the weekend
  • For time phrase: at that time, at night, at an early stage, at an early date etc.

6.Prepositions of Time — Use of On / In

  • 'On' is used for days and dates: on Saturday, on November 25, 2017
  • 'In' is used with the names of month, year, morning, evening and afternoon: in July, in 1985, in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening

7.Use of For, From...to/till, During

'For' is used to denote a period of time, 'from...to/till' is used to denote a beginning point of time and a finishing point of time, 'during' is used to denote all through a period of time.

  • I lived in Delhi for two years.
  • I worked there from February to/till October.
  • He learnt skating during summer vacation.

8.Use of Before, After, Since, Till/Until

These prepositions are used with point of time.

  • We shall return before/after evening/5 o'clock/July.
  • He was born before/after 1985.
  • She has been studying since morning.
  • We waited till/until evening/7:30. (We did not wait after that time.)

Note: 'Till' is more informal than 'until'. In the beginning of a sentence 'until' is used.

9.Use of Between, By, Up to

'Between' is used to denote a starting point of time and a finishing point of time. 'By' is used to denote a point of time, 'up to' is also used to denote a point of time.

  • The shop remains closed between 2 pm and 4 pm.
  • He will finish his work up to next week.
  • They had completed their homework by 6 o'clock.

10.Use of Through, Throughout, Towards, Behind, To, Past

  • The day became cold towards evening. (preposition of time)
  • The army headed towards the border. (preposition of place)
  • Hurry up! We are behind time/schedule. (late)
  • What is the time? It is ten minutes to two. It is ten minutes past two.
  • It was past midnight when we reached home.
  • The patient will not live through night.
  • The museum is open daily throughout the year.

11.Prepositions of Instrument, Agent, Means — By / With

'By' usually denotes the agent or doer; 'with' denotes the instrument.

  • The apple was cut by Riya with a knife.
  • The accident was seen by him with his own eyes.

'By' is also used to signify 'by means of'.

  • They usually travel by a car.

12.Prepositions of Manner

  • She danced with a lot of grace.
  • They played with confidence.

13.Prepositions of Cause, Reason, Purpose

  • I bought this book for you.
  • You should fight for your rights.
  • He died of cancer.
  • She is suffering from typhoid.
  • She failed due to her carelessness.

14.Prepositions of Direction

Prepositions of direction denote direction or movement of persons or things with respect to someone or something else. Examples: to, towards, into, at, for, against, off, around, along, across, up, down etc.

  • He went to market. / The army marched towards the line of control.
  • He jumped into the well. / Look at the sun.
  • He has taken the flight for Delhi. / He leaned against the wall.

15.Prepositions of Possession

  • Don't pull the tail of the dog.
  • There is a man with a sword in his hands.

16.Prepositions of Measure, Rate

  • Cloth is sold by the metre.
  • He drove his car at 100 kmph.
  • Bananas are available at 45 rupees per dozen.
  • I bought this book for rupees 250.

17.Prepositions of Contrast, Concession

  • With all his faults, he was accepted by his family.
  • For all the wrong you have done to her, she loves you.

18.Beneath / Beyond

'Beneath' is used to denote a position lower than somebody or something: They found the treasure beneath the roots of a big tree.

'Beyond' is used:

  • To denote on or to the further side of something: The ball went beyond the boundary.
  • Later than a particular time: I will work on a new project for next six months, but I have not thought what I will do beyond that.

19.The Same Preposition with Many Uses — At / In

At is used:

  • Before towns and villages: They live at Sangaria.
  • For smaller place when two places are mentioned in a sentence: He lives at Ferozepur in Punjab.
  • For big towns or cities to denote a temporary stay: We stopped at Bathinda on the way to Ahmedabad from Jammu.
  • To indicate a point in space: He is waiting for you at the main gate.
  • With the name of building with reference to the activities going on inside: I work at a bank.
  • To indicate direction: Look at the board.
  • To indicate distance away from something: Can you read the board at 15 feet?
  • To indicate a rate, price, speed etc: He ran at a full speed.
  • To indicate order or frequency: She visited her doctor at regular intervals.
  • To denote cause: She was delighted at the thought of winning the lottery.
  • After certain adjectives: He is good at painting.
  • With the name of a person + 's to refer to that person's home or place of work: There is a party at Verma's today.
  • For point of time, chiefly clock time: at 6 o'clock, at noon, at night, at midnight
  • For holiday periods: at Christmas, at the weekend
  • For time phrase: at that time, at night, at an early stage, at an early date etc.

In is used:

  • Before cities, countries and provinces: He lives in India.
  • To indicate a place at a point within the area or volume of something: Nile is the longest river in the world.
  • To indicate a place within the shape of something: He had a ball in his hand. / He is still in bed.
  • To indicate a movement: They went out in the street.
  • To indicate a period of time: I was born in November 1978.
  • In the sense of after (a maximum length of time): They will return in two hours.
  • In the sense of during/for generally after a negative or first, last etc.: I never met him in these four years.
  • To form the whole or part: There are seven days in a week.
  • In the sense of wearing clothes: There was a man in suit.
  • To indicate physical surroundings: Let's sit in the sun. / Don't go out in the rain.
  • To indicate form, shapes, arrangements or quantities: Stand in a row. / Work in groups.
  • To indicate the medium, means, material: She talks in Telugu. / The letter was written in black ink.
  • To indicate the state or condition of somebody or something: She is in deep depression. / We left the room in a hurry.
  • To refer to something: He is blind in one eye. / Carrots are rich in fibre.
  • To introduce a particular person: She found her soul mate in him.
  • To indicate occupation or activity: His father is in politics.

20.In / Into

  • In denotes rest or position inside something: She sat in an armchair.
  • Into denotes motion or direction, change or transformation: He jumped into the river. / Translate this passage into English.

21.To / Into

  • To denotes direction whereas into denotes entry from outside: They went to market. / He went into the room.

22.In / Within / Before

  • In denotes at the end of a period of time: They will return your books in one week. (after the expiry of the period of one week)
  • Within denotes before the expiry of the specified period of time: They will return your books within one week. (before the expiry of the period of one week)
  • Within is also used to denote the boundaries of a place or limits of something: Students must remain within the campus during school hours.
  • Before is used when we specify some time before a point of time/fixed time: They will return before 30th November.

23.On / Upon

On is used:

  • To denote a position covering, touching or forming part of a surface: The cat is on the table. / There is a painting on the wall.
  • To denote supported by or attached to: She hung her coat on a peg. / The flag was hoisted on a high pole.
  • With days and dates: I will come back on Sunday. / My birthday falls on September 15.
  • To denote a point of time or occasion: I congratulated her on getting the first rank in the board examination.
  • To denote concerning or regarding: He has written many books on psychology.
  • To indicate membership of a group or organisation: He was on the selection committee.
  • To denote regular consumption of something: A cow lives on grass. / Most cars run on petrol.
  • To denote direction: You will find the library on your left.
  • To indicate near or close to a place: The city was on the bank of a river.
  • To indicate a basis, ground or reason for something: He was dismissed from the job on the charge of corruption. / On her advice, I changed my decision.
  • To denote financial support: She lived on the little money sent by her brother.
  • To denote by means of, using: Her speech was telecast on television.
  • To indicate an activity, a purpose or a state: He went abroad on a business tour. / They took some money on loan.

Upon is used:

  • To denote things in motion: The cat jumped upon the table. / The lion pounced upon the deer.

24.Above / Over

Above is used:

  • To denote higher than: The water has risen above the mark of danger. / We flew above the clouds.
  • To denote higher in rank, position, importance etc. than: A Principal ranks above a lecturer.
  • To denote greater in number, price, weight etc than: The temperature is above normal today.
  • To denote more than: He values money above relationships.
  • To denote that someone or something is too good or great or beyond the reach of something: His subordinate is above suspicion.

Over is used:

  • To denote resting on the surface of and partly or completely covering: She put a blanket over her ailing mother. / Spread a cover over the table.
  • To denote a higher position than something but not touching: He held an umbrella over his head. / There was a fan over my head.
  • To denote crossing from one side of something to the other; across: She looked over the window. / The children ran over the carpet.
  • To denote that someone or something moves to the other side: He jumped over the wall. / She went over the stream.
  • To denote on the far or opposite side of something: Whose house is that over the street?
  • To denote in or across every part or most parts of some place: It is raining over the mountains. / He is famous all over the world.
  • To denote more than: He is over forty.
  • To indicate command, authority, superiority or control: The British ruled over India for 200 years.
  • To denote throughout or during: They discussed the issue over dinner.
  • To denote because of, concerning: There is a dispute between them over a piece of land.
  • To denote transmitted by: They informed about the accident over telephone.

25.Below / Under / Beneath / Beyond

Below is used:

  • To denote at or to a lower position, level etc.: The water in the river is below the mark of danger. / Her dress is below the knees.

Under is used:

  • To denote in, to or through a position directly below something: The cat is under the chair. / There is a river under the bridge.
  • To denote covered by; below the surface of: He kept the letter under the pillow. / The children hid their faces under the blanket.
  • To denote in or to a position next to and lower than something: They refreshed themselves under the waterfall.
  • To denote younger than a specified age: Nobody under 18 is allowed to buy alcohol.
  • To denote being in a state of: The bridge is under construction. / Your proposal is under consideration.
  • To denote less than: Her monthly income was under ten thousand rupees.
  • To denote who or what controls, governs or manages somebody or something: The country is under the martial law. / She has 50 workers under her.
  • According to an agreement, a law or system: A man was detained under the Arms Act.
  • To denote being affected by: The building collapsed under stress. / She has been under stress lately.

Beneath is used:

  • To denote a position lower than somebody or something: They found the treasure beneath the roots of a big tree.
  • Not good enough for somebody: He considers such jobs beneath him.

Beyond is used:

  • To denote on or to the further side of something: The road went beyond the village up to the hills.
  • Later than a particular time: The rain will continue beyond evening.
  • More than something: Our achievement was far beyond what we had expected.
  • Used to say that something is not possible: His behaviour is beyond improvement.
  • Too far or too advanced for somebody or something: The sums were beyond the understanding of the students.

26.Since / From

Since is used:

  • To denote from a specified time in the past till a later past time, or till now: I have not seen him since November.

From is used:

  • To indicate the place or direction from which some person or something starts: He went from Delhi to Mumbai.
  • To indicate the time at which something starts: They lived in London from 2008 to 2012.
  • To indicate who gave, sent or communicated something: They received a message from the Head Office.
  • To indicate where some person or thing originates, or is stored: This is a famous quotation from Keats.
  • To indicate distance between two places: Her village is ten kilometres from the town.
  • To indicate the range of something: He meets from 15 to 20 people every day. The prices of these trousers start from 150 rupees.
  • To indicate the state or form of something before a change: Translate this letter from English to Hindi.
  • To indicate the material from which something is made: Ghee is made from butter.
  • To indicate separation, removal etc.: The leaves fall from trees in autumn. / Somebody has stolen money from my bag.
  • To indicate protection or prevention: He stopped me from entering the office. / They saved a woman from drowning.
  • To indicate cause or reason, cause or motive: She fell sick from overwork. / They helped us from a sense of compassion.
  • To make distinction between two persons or thing: How will you distinguish salt from sugar? My brother is different from me.

27.With / By

With is used:

  • To denote in the company of or presence of, to denote in the charge or possession of: I go to school with my brother. / She leaves her son with her mother every day.
  • To denote having or carrying: I saw a lady with a black bag in her hand.
  • To indicate a tool or instrument used: Cut the apple with a knife. / I saw it with my own eyes.
  • To indicate the material or item used: The bag was filled with coins.
  • To denote agreement or support: She was with the team on this point. / This hair style goes well with your dress.
  • To denote because of, on account of: He was pale with anaemia.
  • To indicate the manner, circumstances or condition in which something is done or takes place: You must do your work with the whole heart. They welcomed us with open arms.
  • Employed by or using the services of: I bank with OBC bank. / She worked with an insurance company for two years.
  • To indicate separation from: I cannot part with my family. / They dispensed with the formalities.
  • To denote in spite of; despite: With all her faults, he still respected her.

By is used:

  • To denote near, beside, at the side of: They have a house by the river. / Come and sit by me.
  • To denote agent or doer in a passive sentence: He was praised by the teacher. / Othello is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare.
  • To denote across, along, passing through: The thieves entered by the back window. / They travelled to Kerala by Maharashtra and Karnataka.
  • Pass: He walked by me without noticing me.
  • To denote not later than; before: You must submit the report by Saturday. / I shall be in Delhi by 5 o'clock.
  • To denote during: He sleeps by day and works by night.
  • To denote through the means of: Can I pay by cheque? / They contacted me by email.
  • To denote as a result of, because of: He met her by chance. / She forgot the papers at home by mistake.
  • To indicate a means of transport or a route taken: I usually travel by bus.
  • To indicate a part of the body, or an item of clothing touched, held etc.: The policeman took the thief by the collar.
  • To denote using a standard or unit of measurement: Oranges are sold by the dozen.
  • To denote in successive units, groups or degrees of: He is getting weak day by day.
  • To denote according to; from the evidence of: What is the time by your watch?

28.Between / Among

Between is used:

  • To denote shared by two persons or thing: There is a table between the two chairs. The property was divided between two brothers.

Among is used:

  • To indicate division, distribution, possession, or joint activity of, for or by more than two persons: The property was divided among four sisters.
  • To indicate connection, association, inclusion: He was among the first few persons to enter the hall. We saw him among the crowd.

29.Beside / Besides

Beside is used:

  • To mean at, or by, the side of; next to: There is a small park beside my house. / He sat beside me.
  • To denote compared with: He was quite short beside his brother. / Beside your earlier movie, this one seems more well-directed.

Besides is used:

  • To denote except; apart from: No one helped with him besides his friend.
  • To denote in addition to: Besides a gold medal, he was given a cash prize of rupees five thousand.

30.Of / Off

Of is used:

  • To denote belonging to: India is a land of fairs and festivals.
  • To denote originating from or living in: He comes of a noble family.
  • To denote in relation to, concerning or depicting: I need a map of India.
  • To denote about: He had never heard of such places before.
  • To denote the material used to make something: She wore a beautiful dress of silk.
  • To denote so that somebody no longer has, suffers from something: The robbers robbed us of the money we had. Can you cure her of depression?
  • To indicate a cause: She died of cancer.
  • To introduce a phrase that describes a preceding Noun: He is a man of few words. / She is a lady of virtue.
  • To show who is being described by It is/was + Adjective: It is very kind of you to help me.

Off is used:

  • To denote down or away from: He fell off the tree.
  • To denote accessible from: There is a lane off the main road.
  • To denote at some distance from: There is a good restaurant off my house.
  • To denote not wishing or needing to take: She remained off food for a week.

31.For

For is used:

  • To indicate the person intended to receive or benefit from: My father bought a new watch for me.
  • To indicate purpose or function: They went for a picnic. / He looked for his book everywhere.
  • To indicate destination, aim or reason: He has taken the flight for Delhi.
  • To denote as the price, reward or penalty of something: He got Nobel Prize for his contribution to world literature. / I bought this book for five hundred rupees. One has to pay for one's sins.
  • To denote as the replacement of something else: He got two thousand rupees for his old watch.
  • To denote as a representative of: He spoke for all the workers in the factory.
  • Employed by: She works for a private company.
  • In support of somebody or something: Are you for or against the proposal?
  • To denote in order to obtain: He came to me for discussion over a case.
  • To denote a length of time/period of time: He has been playing for two hours. / I will stay here for a week.
  • To denote as the equivalent of, in return for: You will always find a saint for a rogue.
  • To denote with regard to; concerning: He was anxious for his result. / They are eager for us to join them.
  • To show a reason, cause, because of, on account of: For God's sake, don't disturb me. / Please take care of yourself for your family's sake. / The city is famous for its historical monuments.
  • To indicate that something is intended to happen at the specified time: The doctor has given me an appointment for 2nd December. / They got a reservation for the first week of June.
  • To indicate the occasion when something happens: This is for the first time that he has visited this city. / It was for the last time that they were meeting.
  • To indicate a distance: They walked for ten miles to reach the city.
  • To show purpose or design: This presentation is for you to prepare. / It is for you to decide what you want to do in life.

32.Behind / In Front of

Behind is used:

  • To denote at or towards the back of somebody or something, and often hidden by it or them: There is a small temple behind my house.
  • To denote less progress than somebody or something: Hurry up! We are behind schedule.
  • To denote support or approval: Whatever she did, her family always stood up behind her.
  • To denote responsibility for starting or developing something: He was the man behind starting this new project.
  • Used to denote that something is in somebody's past: Our Principal has a long experience behind her.

In Front of is used:

  • To denote the position of somebody or something before someone or something. It means the same as outside but not opposite: He parked the car in front of my house.
  • To denote that something is done in someone's presence: Please don't use such language in front of children.
  • Used to denote time which is yet to come; not yet passed: Don't give up in the face of adversity. You have your whole life in front of you.

43.Quick Revision Checklist

  • A preposition sits before a noun or pronoun and links it to another word in the sentence.
  • The five types are Simple, Compound, Double, Participle, and Phrase prepositions.
  • Place/direction can be seen as a point, line, surface, area, volume, or relative position (vertical vs horizontal).
  • At = point, On = surface, In = enclosed space — the master rule for place and time alike.
  • Time prepositions split by point-of-time (at, on, by, between) vs period-of-time (for, during, since, from...to).
  • By usually marks the agent/doer; With usually marks the instrument.
  • In/Into, To/Into, and In/Within/Before all turn on rest-vs-motion or before-vs-after an expiry point.
  • Above/Over and Below/Under/Beneath/Beyond each cover several distinct relative-position senses — review the full lists, not just one example each.
  • Between is for exactly two; Among is for more than two.
  • Of/Off, Beside/Besides, and Since/From are easy to mix up — check the "many uses" lists above before choosing.
  • Many verbs, adjectives and nouns take one fixed preposition (afraid of, good at, married to, key to) — these are worth memorising as fixed pairs from the 120-word reference list.
