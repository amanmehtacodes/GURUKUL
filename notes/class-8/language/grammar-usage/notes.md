## The Noun

A **noun** is a word that names a person, place, thing, animal, idea, or quality.

**Kinds of Nouns**

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Common Noun | A general name for any person/place/thing | boy, city, river, book |
| Proper Noun | The particular name of a person/place/thing (always capitalised) | Ravi, Delhi, Ganga, Taj Mahal |
| Collective Noun | Names a group of people/things as one unit | team, herd, jury, bunch, fleet |
| Material Noun | Names the substance or material something is made of | gold, wood, water, cotton |
| Abstract Noun | Names a quality, state, feeling, or idea that cannot be touched | honesty, courage, joy, childhood |
| Countable Noun | Can be counted; has singular and plural forms | book/books, chair/chairs |
| Uncountable Noun | Cannot be counted; has no plural form | milk, information, furniture, advice |

**Gender of Nouns**

| Masculine | Feminine | Common (either) | Neuter (neither) |
|---|---|---|---|
| king | queen | student | table |
| lion | lioness | teacher | book |
| actor | actress | friend | mountain |

**Number of Nouns** — rules for forming plurals:

- Most nouns: add **-s** → book → books
- Nouns ending in s, x, ch, sh, z: add **-es** → box → boxes, bus → buses
- Nouns ending in consonant + y: change **y to i** and add **-es** → city → cities, baby → babies
- Nouns ending in vowel + y: add **-s** → boy → boys, day → days
- Nouns ending in f/fe: change to **v** and add **-es** → leaf → leaves, knife → knives (exceptions: roof → roofs, chief → chiefs)
- Irregular plurals: man → men, woman → women, child → children, tooth → teeth, foot → feet, mouse → mice, ox → oxen
- Same in singular and plural: sheep, deer, fish, series, species, aircraft
- Always plural: scissors, trousers, spectacles, jeans

**Case of Nouns**

- **Nominative** (subject of the verb): *Rina* sings well.
- **Objective/Accusative** (object of the verb): The teacher praised *Rina*.
- **Possessive/Genitive** (shows ownership): *Rina's* voice is sweet.

**Possessive apostrophe rules:** add **'s** to singular nouns (the boy's bag); add only **'** to plural nouns ending in -s (the boys' bag); add **'s** to irregular plurals (the children's toys).

> **Quick Practice:** Identify the noun and its kind — "The **jury** delivered its verdict after examining the **evidence**." (jury — collective; evidence — abstract/uncountable)

## The Pronoun

A **pronoun** is a word used in place of a noun, to avoid repeating it.

| Kind | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | Refers to specific people/things | I, you, he, she, it, we, they, me, him, her, us, them |
| Possessive | Shows ownership | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| Reflexive | Refers back to the subject | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves |
| Emphatic | Emphasises the noun/pronoun (same form as reflexive) | I myself saw it happen. |
| Demonstrative | Points to specific things | this, that, these, those |
| Interrogative | Used to ask questions | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| Relative | Joins a clause to a noun/pronoun | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| Indefinite | Refers to non-specific people/things | someone, anybody, everything, none, few, all, each |

**Common errors to watch for**

- Use **who** for the subject, **whom** for the object: *Who* is calling? / To *whom* did you give it?
- **Myself, yourself,** etc. should not replace **I/me** or **you** as ordinary subjects/objects: ~~Myself and Ravi went~~ → *Ravi and I went.*
- A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in **number and gender**: Every student must submit *his/her* project (not *their*, in formal usage, though *their* is now widely accepted as gender-neutral singular).

> **Quick Practice:** Fill in the blank — "Neither Priya nor her sisters had brought _____ books." (their — the pronoun agrees with the nearer noun, *sisters*)

## The Adjective

An **adjective** is a word that qualifies or describes a noun or pronoun.

| Kind | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Adjective of Quality | Describes kind/quality | tall, beautiful, honest, blue |
| Adjective of Quantity | Tells how much | some, much, little, enough, no |
| Adjective of Number | Tells how many / order | five, several, first, many, few |
| Demonstrative Adjective | Points out which one | this book, those chairs |
| Interrogative Adjective | Asks a question before a noun | Which book? Whose pen? |
| Possessive Adjective | Shows possession | my, your, his, her, its, our, their |

**Degrees of Comparison**

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| tall | taller | tallest |
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |
| good | better | best |
| bad | worse | worst |
| little | less | least |
| many/much | more | most |

**Rules for forming comparatives/superlatives:**

- One-syllable words: add **-er/-est** (tall → taller → tallest)
- Words ending in **e**: add **-r/-st** (large → larger → largest)
- Words ending in consonant + y: change **y to i**, add **-er/-est** (happy → happier → happiest)
- Two-or-more-syllable words: use **more/most** (careful → more careful → most careful)
- Never combine **-er** with **more**, or **-est** with **most**: ~~more taller~~, ~~most tallest~~ are incorrect.

> **Quick Practice:** Correct the error — "This is the *more best* solution." → "This is the *best* solution."

## Determiners

A **determiner** is a word placed before a noun to make its reference clear — it tells us which one, whose, or how many/much.

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Articles | a, an, the |
| Demonstratives | this, that, these, those |
| Possessives | my, your, his, her, its, our, their |
| Quantifiers | some, any, no, many, much, few, little, several, all, both |
| Numbers | one, two, first, second |

**Articles — a/an vs the**

- **a/an** (indefinite articles) are used before singular countable nouns when mentioned for the first time or in a general sense: *a* dog, *an* umbrella.
  - Use **a** before words beginning with a consonant *sound*: a university (sounds like "yoo"), a one-rupee coin.
  - Use **an** before words beginning with a vowel *sound*: an hour (silent h), an MBA.
- **the** (definite article) is used when the noun refers to something specific, already mentioned, or unique: *the* sun, *the* Prime Minister of India, I saw a dog; *the* dog was black.
- **No article** is used with plural/uncountable nouns in a general sense (Milk is healthy) and with most proper nouns (India, Mount Everest — though some proper nouns like rivers, seas, and mountain ranges do take "the": the Ganga, the Himalayas).

**Some/Any:** *some* is generally used in affirmative sentences (I have some money); *any* is used in negative sentences and questions (I don't have any money. Do you have any money?).

**Few/A few, Little/A little:** *a few/a little* mean "some, a small amount" (positive sense); *few/little* (without "a") mean "hardly any" (negative sense).
- I have *a few* friends here. (some, positively noted)
- I have *few* friends here. (almost none)

## The Verb

A **verb** is a word that expresses an action, occurrence, or state of being.

**Kinds of Verbs**

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Transitive | Takes a direct object | She *wrote* a letter. |
| Intransitive | Does not take a direct object | The baby *sleeps*. |
| Auxiliary (Helping) | Helps the main verb form tense/voice/mood | is, am, are, was, were, has, have, had, do, does, did |
| Modal | Expresses ability, possibility, permission, obligation | can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would |
| Linking | Connects the subject to a complement | be, seem, become, appear, look, feel, taste |

**Subject–Verb Agreement basics** (see also the dedicated section below): a singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.

**Regular vs Irregular Verbs**

- Regular verbs form the past tense/past participle by adding **-ed**: walk → walked → walked.
- Irregular verbs change form unpredictably: go → went → gone; eat → ate → eaten; write → wrote → written; see → saw → seen.

## Finite and Non-Finite Verbs

A **finite verb** changes according to the number and person of its subject and shows tense. It can stand as the main verb of a sentence.
- She *writes* a letter every day. (finite — agrees with "she", shows present tense)
- They *wrote* letters yesterday. (finite — shows past tense)

A **non-finite verb** does not change according to the subject and does not show tense by itself. It cannot be the sole verb of a sentence. There are three kinds:

| Kind | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | to + base verb | *To succeed* requires hard work. |
| Gerund | verb + -ing (acts as a noun) | *Swimming* is good exercise. |
| Participle | present (-ing) or past (-ed/-en) form used as an adjective | The *crying* baby fell asleep. / *Broken* glass lay on the floor. |

> **Distinguishing gerund from present participle:** a gerund functions as a noun (subject/object), while a present participle functions as an adjective or forms a continuous tense.
> - *Reading* is my hobby. (gerund — subject)
> - I saw him *reading*. (present participle — describes "him", part of continuous sense)

**Common exam task:** identify the non-finite verb form and its type in a sentence, e.g., "*Having finished* his homework, Raj went out to play." → *Having finished* is a perfect participle.

## Modal Auxiliaries

Modals are helping verbs that express ability, permission, possibility, necessity, or obligation. They are followed by the **base form** of the main verb (no "to", no -s, -ed, -ing).

| Modal | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| can | ability, informal permission | She *can* swim. Can I leave early? |
| could | past ability, polite request, possibility | He *could* run fast as a child. Could you help me? |
| may | permission, possibility | May I come in? It *may* rain today. |
| might | weaker possibility | It *might* rain later. |
| must | strong obligation, certainty | You *must* wear a helmet. He *must* be tired. |
| should/ought to | advice, mild obligation | You *should* apologise. |
| shall | formal future (with I/we), offers | I *shall* return tomorrow. Shall I help you? |
| will | future, willingness, promise | I *will* call you. |
| would | polite request, past habit, conditional | Would you please sit down? He *would* visit us every summer. |
| need not | absence of obligation | You *need not* come if you're busy. |

**Common confusions**

- **Can vs May:** *can* asks about ability or informal permission; *may* is the more formal/polite way to ask permission.
- **Must vs Have to:** *must* often expresses the speaker's own authority/obligation; *have to* expresses an external obligation/rule.
- **Mustn't vs Need not:** *mustn't* means "it is forbidden"; *need not* means "it is not necessary" — these are **not** interchangeable negatives of "must".

> **Quick Practice:** Choose the correct modal — "It is cloudy. It ___ rain today." (may/might — expresses possibility, not certainty)

## Tenses

Tense shows the **time** of an action. English has three main time frames (Present, Past, Future), each with four aspects (Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous) — 12 tenses in all.

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | V1(s/es) | She *walks* to school. |
| Present Continuous | is/am/are + V-ing | She *is walking* to school. |
| Present Perfect | has/have + V3 | She *has walked* to school. |
| Present Perfect Continuous | has/have been + V-ing | She *has been walking* for an hour. |
| Simple Past | V2 | She *walked* to school. |
| Past Continuous | was/were + V-ing | She *was walking* when it rained. |
| Past Perfect | had + V3 | She *had walked* before the bell rang. |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had been + V-ing | She *had been walking* for an hour before she stopped. |
| Simple Future | shall/will + V1 | She *will walk* to school. |
| Future Continuous | shall/will + be + V-ing | She *will be walking* at 8 a.m. |
| Future Perfect | shall/will + have + V3 | She *will have walked* by then. |
| Future Perfect Continuous | shall/will + have been + V-ing | She *will have been walking* for an hour by 9 a.m. |

**When to use which — quick guide**

- **Simple Present:** habits, facts, timetables, general truths. The sun *rises* in the east.
- **Present Continuous:** actions happening right now, or fixed future arrangements. I *am reading* this book currently. We *are leaving* tomorrow.
- **Present Perfect:** past action with a present result/relevance, no specific time stated. I *have finished* my homework.
- **Simple Past:** completed action at a definite past time. He *visited* Agra last year.
- **Past Continuous:** an action in progress at a point in the past, often interrupted by another action. I *was watching* TV when the phone rang.
- **Past Perfect:** an action completed before another past action ("past of the past"). The train *had left* before I reached the station.
- **Simple Future:** predictions, spontaneous decisions, promises. It *will rain* tomorrow.

**Time markers that hint at tense:** always, usually, every day (Simple Present); now, at the moment, currently (Continuous); already, yet, just, since, for (Perfect); yesterday, ago, last week (Simple Past); by the time, before (Past/Future Perfect).

**News headline tenses (a special case):** headlines compress language for impact and often use different tenses from ordinary prose:
- **Simple Present** for a recent past event: *Sub-Inspector Found Dead* → A sub-inspector *was found* dead...
- **Infinitive with "to"** for a future action: *PM likely to flag off engine* → The PM *is likely to flag off*...
- **Past Participle** for a passive action: *8 Killed in Accident* → Eight people *were killed*...
- **Present Participle** for an ongoing/future situation: *GST having negative effect* → GST *has had* a negative effect...

## The Adverb

An **adverb** modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb — it usually answers *how, when, where,* or *to what extent*.

| Kind | Question Answered | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Adverb of Manner | How? | slowly, carefully, beautifully, well |
| Adverb of Time | When? | now, yesterday, soon, already |
| Adverb of Place | Where? | here, there, everywhere, outside |
| Adverb of Frequency | How often? | always, often, never, usually, sometimes |
| Adverb of Degree | To what extent? | very, quite, too, almost, extremely |

**Formation:** most manner adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective: quick → quickly, careful → carefully. (Exceptions: good → well; fast, hard, late remain the same in adjective and adverb forms.)

**Position of adverbs:**
- Adverbs of manner usually come **after** the verb/object: She sang *beautifully*.
- Adverbs of frequency usually come **before** the main verb but **after** "be": He *always* arrives on time. She *is often* late.
- Adverbs of time/place usually come at the **end** of the sentence: I will meet you *tomorrow*.

> **Quick Practice:** Identify the adverb and its kind — "He *rarely* speaks in class." (rarely — adverb of frequency)

## Prepositions

A **preposition** shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another word in the sentence — usually relating to place, time, direction, or manner.

| Category | Common Prepositions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Time | at, on, in, since, for, before, after, during | at 5 o'clock, on Monday, in June, since 2020 |
| Place | at, on, in, under, over, above, below, between, among | at the door, on the table, in the box |
| Direction | to, into, onto, towards, through, across | walk *into* the room, drive *across* the bridge |
| Manner/Agency | by, with | written *by* him, cut *with* a knife |

**Common confusions**

- **In/At/On (time):** *in* for months/years/seasons (in May, in 2020); *on* for days/dates (on Monday, on 5th May); *at* for precise clock time (at 6 p.m.).
- **In/At/On (place):** *in* for enclosed spaces (in the room); *at* for a point (at the bus stop); *on* for a surface (on the table).
- **Between vs Among:** *between* is used for two people/things; *among* is used for more than two.
- **Prepositions with fixed verbs/adjectives (collocations):** good *at*, afraid *of*, interested *in*, married *to*, angry *with* (a person), angry *at* (a situation), depend *on*, listen *to*, arrive *at/in*.

> **Quick Practice:** Fill in the blank — "She is good ___ Mathematics." (at)

## Conjunctions

A **conjunction** joins words, phrases, or clauses.

| Kind | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinating | Joins equal/independent elements | and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet |
| Subordinating | Joins a dependent clause to a main clause | because, although, since, if, when, while, unless, so that |
| Correlative | Work in pairs | either...or, neither...nor, both...and, not only...but also, whether...or |

**Usage notes**

- **And** adds information; **but** shows contrast; **or** shows a choice/alternative; **so** shows a result; **because/since/as** show reason; **although/though** show contrast/concession; **if/unless** show condition.
- With **correlative conjunctions**, the verb agrees with the noun **nearer to it**: *Neither the teacher nor the students **were*** present. *Neither the students nor the teacher **was*** present.
- Avoid starting a formal sentence with "And" or "But" in written composition, though this rule is relaxed in informal writing.

## Subject-Verb Agreement

The verb must agree with its subject in **number** (singular/plural) and **person**.

**Key rules**

1. A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb: The *dog barks*. The *dogs bark*.
2. Two subjects joined by **and** usually take a plural verb: *Ram and Shyam are* friends. (Exception: if the two nouns refer to a single idea/person — *Bread and butter is* my breakfast.)
3. Subjects joined by **or/nor/either...or/neither...nor** take a verb that agrees with the **nearer** subject: *Either the boys or the girl **is*** responsible.
4. **Collective nouns** (team, family, jury, committee) take a singular verb when acting as one unit, and a plural verb when members act individually: The *team is* playing well. The *team are* divided in their opinions.
5. Words like **each, every, either, neither, everyone, everybody, someone, nobody** are always singular: *Everybody is* invited. *Each of the students has* a book.
6. A phrase between subject and verb does not change the verb's number: The *box of chocolates **is*** on the table (not "are" — the subject is "box", not "chocolates").
7. **Uncountable nouns** (news, furniture, information, advice) always take a singular verb: The *news is* shocking.
8. Nouns that look plural but are singular in meaning (mathematics, physics, news, measles) take a singular verb: *Mathematics is* my favourite subject.
9. **There is/There are:** agreement depends on the noun that follows: *There is* a book on the table. *There are* books on the table.

> **Quick Practice:** Correct the error — "Each of the boys *have* done well." → "Each of the boys *has* done well."

## Active and Passive Voice

**Voice** shows whether the subject of a sentence performs the action (**Active**) or receives the action (**Passive**).

- Active: *Subject + Verb + Object* → **He** *wrote* a letter.
- Passive: *Object + be + Past Participle + by + Subject* → A letter *was written* **by him**.

**Rules for changing Active to Passive**

1. The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.
2. The verb changes to **a form of "be" + past participle (V3)**, matching the original tense.
3. The subject of the active sentence becomes the **agent**, introduced by **"by"** (often dropped if unimportant or unknown).
4. The tense of the sentence must be preserved.

**Passive forms across tenses**

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | He writes a letter. | A letter is written by him. |
| Present Continuous | He is writing a letter. | A letter is being written by him. |
| Present Perfect | He has written a letter. | A letter has been written by him. |
| Simple Past | He wrote a letter. | A letter was written by him. |
| Past Continuous | He was writing a letter. | A letter was being written by him. |
| Past Perfect | He had written a letter. | A letter had been written by him. |
| Simple Future | He will write a letter. | A letter will be written by him. |
| Future Perfect | He will have written a letter. | A letter will have been written by him. |
| Modal (can/must, etc.) | He can write a letter. | A letter can be written by him. |

**Special cases**

- Sentences with two objects: either object can become the subject of the passive. He gave *her* *a book* → *She was given a book* / *A book was given to her*.
- Imperative sentences: "Close the door" → "Let the door be closed" / "You are requested to close the door."
- Interrogative sentences: "Did he write the letter?" → "Was the letter written by him?"
- When the doer is unknown or unimportant, "by + agent" is dropped: "The road is being repaired" (not "by the workers").

**Why passive voice matters for this syllabus:** it is frequently used for **describing a process** (recipes, instructions, "How to make..." passages), since the focus is on the action, not who does it: *First, the vegetables are washed. Then they are chopped into small pieces.*

## Narration (Direct and Indirect Speech)

**Direct speech** repeats the speaker's exact words in quotation marks. **Indirect (reported) speech** reports what was said without quoting the exact words.

**Rules for converting Direct to Indirect Speech**

1. **Remove the quotation marks** and use a connector (that, if/whether, or a question word).
2. **Reporting verb changes:** "says/say" → "says"; "said" → "said"; "says to / say to" → "tells/tell"; "said to" → "told".
3. **Tense shifts back** (if the reporting verb is in the past tense):

| Direct (in quotes) | Indirect |
|---|---|
| Simple Present | Simple Past |
| Present Continuous | Past Continuous |
| Present Perfect | Past Perfect |
| Simple Past | Past Perfect |
| Present Perfect Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous |
| will/shall | would/should |
| can | could |
| may | might |

4. **Pronouns change** according to sense (I → he/she, we → they, my → his/her, etc.).
5. **Time and place words change:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tomorrow | the next day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| ago | before |
| next week | the following week |

**Narration of different sentence types**

- **Statements:** use **that** as the connector. He said, "I am happy." → He said (that) he was happy.
- **Questions:** use **if/whether** for yes-no questions; use the **question word** (what, where, why...) for wh-questions; drop the auxiliary-subject inversion (word order becomes statement-like) and change "say/said" to "ask/asked". He said, "Where do you live?" → He asked where I lived. He said, "Are you coming?" → He asked if I was coming.
- **Commands/Requests:** use **to + infinitive** for orders, **not to + infinitive** for negative orders; reporting verb becomes ordered/told/requested/advised. She said, "Sit down." → She told me to sit down. He said, "Please help me." → He requested me to help him.
- **Exclamations:** the reporting verb becomes exclaimed with joy/sorrow/wonder, etc., and the exclamation mark is removed. He said, "What a beautiful scene!" → He exclaimed that it was a very beautiful scene.

> **Quick Practice:** Convert — "She said to me, 'Where did you go yesterday?'" → She asked me where I had gone the previous day.

## Integrated Grammar Practice

This section brings multiple grammar skills together in exam-style formats. Recognising the *format* of each task is half the battle.

**1. Sentence Reordering (Jumbled Words):** rearrange scrambled words/phrases into a meaningful sentence. Strategy: find the subject first, then look for the verb, then arrange objects/modifiers in natural order.
> good / without / life / hardly / worth living / health / is → *Life is hardly worth living without good health.*

**2. News Headlines — Interpreting and Expanding:** headlines use compressed grammar (see the Tenses section above for the rules). To expand a headline into a sentence, restore the full subject, correct tense/voice, and add missing articles/prepositions.
> "Sub-Inspector Found Dead in Jandiala" → A Punjab police sub-inspector was found shot dead near the district headquarters on Monday morning.

**3. Dialogue Completion:** fill in a missing half of a conversation, keeping tense, tone, and question-answer logic consistent with what is given.

**4. Cloze / Gap-Filling:** choose the correct word (article, preposition, verb form, conjunction) from options to complete a passage. Read the whole sentence (sometimes the whole passage) before choosing, since the answer often depends on words later in the sentence.

**5. Editing — Error Correction:** one word in each line is wrong; identify and correct it. Common error types: wrong tense, wrong preposition, wrong article, singular/plural mismatch, wrong parts of speech (e.g., "prize" for "prized").
> "The Taj Mahal was **build** by the Mughal Emperor..." → incorrect: *build*, correct: *built*

**6. Editing — Omission:** one word has been left out of each line; supply it along with the word before and after it.
> "One ⌃ Kenya's greatest athletes" → preceding: *One*, omitted: *of*, succeeding: *Kenya's*

**7. Describing a Process (Passive Voice):** convert a set of instructions (imperative, numbered steps) into a flowing paragraph using the **passive voice**, since the focus is on the process, not the person doing it. Choose present, past, or future tense as instructed by the question.
> "Beat the cream. Add sugar." → *The cream is beaten. Sugar is added to it.*

**Exam tip:** for every integrated-grammar task, first identify which single grammar rule is being tested (tense? preposition? voice? agreement?) — this narrows down the correct option quickly even under time pressure.

| Word | Synonym | Word | Synonym |
|---|---|---|---|

| brief | short | reluctantly | unwillingly |
| conceal | hide | tremble | shake |
| deceive | cheat | vast | huge |

**Antonyms** — words with opposite meanings.

| Word | Antonym | Word | Antonym |
|---|---|---|---|

**Homophones** — same pronunciation, different spelling and meaning:
allowed/aloud, bare/bear, break/brake, fair/fare, hear/here, principal/principle, right/write, sea/see, wood/would.

**Homographs** — same spelling, different meaning (sometimes different pronunciation):
bank (financial institution / river's edge), bat (cricket bat / flying mammal), fair (just / a festival), lie (falsehood / to recline), rose (the flower / past tense of "rise").

**Foreign words commonly used in English:** ad hoc (for this purpose), bona fide (in good faith), curriculum vitae (a summary of one's career), status quo (the existing state of affairs), per annum (per year), lingua franca (a common language).

## Assessment and Exam Practice

Grammar and vocabulary are usually tested in **Section C (Grammar)** of the exam paper, typically worth 20 marks, made up of short objective and application-based questions.

**Typical question types you should be ready for**

1. **Editing (error correction)** — one wrong word per line; write the incorrect and correct word.
2. **Editing (omission)** — one missing word per line; write it with its preceding and succeeding word.
3. **Gap filling with options** — choose the grammatically correct word from four choices (tests articles, prepositions, tense, modals).
4. **Dialogue completion** — fill in a natural, grammatically correct half of a conversation.
5. **Sentence reordering** — arrange jumbled words into a correct sentence.
6. **Voice change (active ↔ passive)** — usually given as an MCQ; check tense and subject-verb agreement in the options carefully.
7. **Narration change (direct ↔ indirect)** — usually given as an MCQ; check pronoun, tense, and time-word shifts.
8. **Modal auxiliary selection** — choose the modal that best expresses the intended meaning (ability, permission, obligation, possibility).
9. **Process description** — convert instructions into a paragraph using passive voice, in the tense specified by the question.
10. **News headline explanation** — expand a compressed headline into one or two full, grammatically correct sentences, inventing reasonable details if asked.

**General exam strategy**

- Read the full sentence/passage before filling any blank — later words often determine the correct earlier choice.
- For MCQs, eliminate options that break subject-verb agreement or use the wrong tense before considering meaning.
- In editing exercises, read each line aloud (silently) — errors often "sound" wrong even before you can name the rule.
- Keep an eye on **one mark per blank** questions — do not leave any blank unattempted, since an educated guess based on grammar patterns is better than a blank answer.
- After finishing, re-read your answers for **subject-verb agreement**, **tense consistency**, and **spelling** — these are the three most common sources of lost marks.
