6.Quick Revision Checklist

  • For one word substitution, focus on the key clue word in the phrase ("one who...", "that which...", "a place where...") — it usually points straight to the right root word.
  • Idioms carry a figurative meaning that has nothing to do with the literal words — never translate them word for word.
  • Proverbs teach a general lesson about life; when matching, ask "what advice is this giving?" rather than looking for literal overlap in wording.
  • For commonly misspelt words, watch for doubled consonants (accommodate, embarrass), silent letters (lieutenant, pneumonia), and "ie" vs "ei" (believe, perceive).
  • Foreign words and phrases keep their original spelling — note which language each comes from, since that often hints at pronunciation.
