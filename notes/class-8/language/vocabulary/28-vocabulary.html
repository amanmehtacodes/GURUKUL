4.Homophones

Homophones are the words that have the same pronunciation, but different spelling and meaning.

  1. Ad: A short form of the word advertisement
    Add: A short form of the word addition
  2. Ail: To be ill or sick
    Ale: A beer
  3. Allowed: That which is permitted
    Aloud: Spoken in a loud manner
  4. Alter: A raised platform used as a place of worship
    Alter: To change
  5. Ate: Past Tense of the word eat
    Eight: The number that comes after seven and before nine
  6. Bail: Temporary release of an accused from the jail
    Bale: a bundle of hay or cotton
  7. Bare: Naked or without any covering
    Bear: A large heavy animal with thick fur
  8. Berth: a seat in a train
    Birth: the beginning of life
  9. Bite: Use teeth to cut something into pieces
    Byte: A group of binary digits or bits
  10. Break: To divide something into pieces

  11. Carat: A unit of weight for precious stones and pearls
    Carrot: A tapering orange-coloured root eaten as a vegetable
  12. Cell: Smallest unit of life
    Sell: Give or hand over in exchange of money
  13. Coarse: Rough or harsh in texture
    Course: The route or direction followed by a ship, aircraft, road or river
  14. Compliment: A polite expression of praise or admiration
    Complement: A thing that contributes extra features to something else
  15. Fair: Treating people equally, just or appropriate in the circumstances
    Fare: The money a passenger on public transport has to pay
  16. Hail: Pellets of frozen rain falling in showers
    Hale: strong and healthy
  17. Heal: To cure a disease
    Heel: The hind part of the foot
  18. Idle: Avoiding work, lazy
    Idol: An image or representation of a god used as an object of worship
  19. Knead: To work dough or clay with hands

  20. Lessen: To reduce or bring down in number

  21. Our: belonging to speaker and one or more persons
    Hour: a unit of time equal to sixty minutes

  25. Principal: the head of a school

  26. Root: A part of a plant which grows underground in the soil
    Route: A way or course taken in getting from a starting point to a destination
  27. Story: An account of imaginary or real people and events told for entertainment

5.Homographs

A homograph is a word with the same spelling and pronunciation used in a different meaning.

  1. Arms: His arms are very thin.
    Arms: The soldiers laid down their arms.
  2. Bat: He has bought a new cricket bat.
    Bat: There is a bat on this tree.
  3. Box: What is there in this box?
    Box: The fighters will box tomorrow.
  4. Bank: My father works in a bank.
    Bank: There was a mango tree on the bank of the river.
  5. Bulb: The light bulb was invented by Edison.
    Bulb: We planted a rose bulb.
  6. Bed: My bed is made of wood.
    Bed: Life is not a bed of roses.
  7. Coat: He is wearing a coat.
    Coat: She applied two coats of nail paint.
  8. Fast: A horse runs very fast.
    Fast: My mother is on fast today.
  9. File: You should file all these papers.
    File: You should file your nails.
  10. Fly: A fly landed on his hand.
    Fly: Birds fly in the sky.
  11. Jam: I like bread with apple jam.
    Jam: He was held up in a traffic jam.
  12. Kind: We should be kind to animals.
    Kind: This is not a good kind of cloth.
  13. Last: This is the last chapter in the book.
    Last: The meeting will last for two hours.
  14. Match: I need a match box.
    Match: They played a cricket match.
  15. Lie: We should never tell a lie.
    Lie: You can lie down here.
  16. Park: There is a park in front of my house.
    Park: You cannot park your car here.
  17. Pool: We have a pool table in our school.
    Pool: There is a swimming pool in his mansion.
  18. Rose: The rose is a pretty flower.
    Rose: The crowd rose to greet the queen.
  19. Train: We travelled by a train.
    Train: The coach will train the players.
  20. Well: He jumped into the well.
    Well: This article is very well-written.

6.Quick Revision Checklist

  • A synonym means the same as another word; an antonym means the opposite — don't mix the two up when a question asks for one or the other.
  • A prefix goes at the front of a word and often reverses or negates its meaning (un-, dis-, im-, il-, ir-, in-, non-); learn a few prefixes well rather than all of them shallowly.
  • A suffix goes at the end of a word and usually changes its part of speech — verb to noun (move → movement), adjective to noun (able → ability), noun to adjective (danger → dangerous).
  • Homophones sound the same but are spelled differently and mean different things — always check the sentence's meaning before choosing the spelling.
  • Homographs are spelled the same but mean different things depending on context — read the whole sentence, not just the word, to know which meaning applies.
  • When in doubt in a fill-in-the-blank exercise, try each option in the sentence out loud — the one that makes sense is usually the answer.
