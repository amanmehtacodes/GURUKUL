## 13. POWER LINES — VISUALISING POWERS

### 🔑 Definition
A **"power line"** is a number line arrangement of consecutive powers of a base, useful for visually confirming multiplication/division rules for exponents.

### 📖 Explanation — Power Line for 4
```
4⁻² 4⁻¹ 4⁰ 4¹ 4² 4³ 4⁴ 4⁵ 4⁶ 4⁷ 4⁸
1/16 1/4  1  4  16 64 256 1024 4096 16384 65536
```
Moving right multiplies by 4; moving left divides by 4.

**Example check:** 16384 (4⁷) is how many times larger than 1024 (4⁵)?
4⁷ ÷ 4⁵ = 4² = 16. **Yes, 16384 is 16 times larger than 1024.**

### 📖 Power Line for 7
```
7⁻⁴  7⁻³  7⁻²  7⁻¹  7⁰  7¹  7²  7³   7⁴   7⁵    7⁶    7⁷
1/2401 1/343 1/49 1/7  1   7   49  343  2401 16807 117649 823543
```

### ❓ Q&A
**Q1. How many times larger than 4⁻² is 4²?**
A. 4² ÷ 4⁻² = 4²⁻⁽⁻²⁾ = 4⁴ = **256 times**. *(Note: textbook context suggests checking the specific power-line values; general rule: nᵃ ÷ nᵇ = nᵃ⁻ᵇ applies here too.)*

**Q2. Using the power line for 7, verify: 2401 × 49 = 7⁶**
A. 2401=7⁴, 49=7². 7⁴×7²=7⁶=117649. ✓ Matches 2401×49=117649.

**Q3. Using the power line for 7, verify: 343 × 2401 = 7⁷**
A. 343=7³, 2401=7⁴. 7³×7⁴=7⁷=823543. ✓

**Q4. Using the power line for 7, simplify 16807/49.**
A. 16807=7⁵, 49=7². 7⁵÷7²=7³=**343**

**Q5. Using the power line for 7, simplify 343/16807.**
A. 7³÷7⁵=7⁻²=**1/49**

**Q6. Using the power line for 7, simplify 823543/117649.**
A. 7⁷÷7⁶=7¹=**7**

**Q7. Using the power line for 7, simplify (1/343)×(1/343).**
A. 7⁻³×7⁻³=7⁻⁶=**1/117649**

**Q8 (own). Using the power line concept for base 3, find how many times larger 3⁶ is than 3².**
A. 3⁶÷3²=3⁴=**81 times**

## 15. SCIENTIFIC NOTATION (STANDARD FORM)

### 🔑 Definition
**Scientific notation** (or **standard form**) writes any number as:
> **x × 10ʸ**, where **1 ≤ x < 10** (x is the coefficient) and **y is any integer** (the exponent).

### 📖 Explanation
- 5900 can be written many ways: 590×10¹, 59×10², 5.9×10³, 0.59×10⁴ — but only **5.9×10³** satisfies 1≤x<10, so this is the standard/scientific form.
- Large numbers become easy to compare: since exponents tell us the "size class" of a number, changes to the exponent matter far more than changes to the coefficient.
  - E.g., Mumbai's population 2×10⁷: if the "2" becomes "3," population grows by 50% (2 crore→3 crore). But if the "7" becomes "8," population grows by 10× (2 crore→20 crore)!
- Scientific notation also communicates **precision**: writing 1.42×10⁵ suggests more certainty than 1.4×10⁵ (fewer significant digits = less claimed precision).

### 📖 Worked Examples
- 20800 = **2.08 × 10⁴**
- 80,00,000 = **8 × 10⁶**
- Distance Sun–Saturn: 14,33,50,00,00,000 m = **1.4335 × 10¹² m**
- Distance Sun–Earth: 1,49,60,00,00,000 m = **1.496 × 10¹¹ m** (smallest of the three distances discussed)

### ❓ Q&A
**Q1. Which of the three distances (Sun-Saturn, Saturn-Uranus, Sun-Earth) is the smallest?**
A. **Distance between Sun and Earth** (1.496×10¹¹ m) — it has the smallest exponent (10¹¹ vs 10¹²).

**Q2. Express 59,853 in standard form.**
A. **5.9853 × 10⁴**

**Q3. Express 65,950 in standard form.**
A. **6.595 × 10⁴**

**Q4. Express 34,30,000 in standard form.**
A. **3.43 × 10⁶**

**Q5. Express 70,04,00,00,000 in standard form.**
A. **7.004 × 10¹⁰**

**Q6 (own). Express 452000 in standard form.**

**Q7 (own). Which represents a bigger number: 6.2×10⁸ or 5.9×10⁹? Explain using the exponent-priority idea.**
A. **5.9×10⁹** is bigger — even though 5.9<6.2, the exponent (9 vs 8) is more significant, since a higher exponent means one more multiplication by 10 (10× larger scale).

## 16. DID YOU EVER WONDER? — ESTIMATION & ASSUMPTIONS

### 🔑 Definition
**Fermi estimation** (the technique used here, though the book doesn't name it) is a method of arriving at a reasonably close estimate for a real-world quantity by:
1. **Guessing** first (instinctive, no calculation)
2. **Describing relationships** between known/unknown quantities
3. **Making reasonable assumptions** where exact data is unavailable
4. **Computing** the estimate and comparing it to the initial guess

### 📖 Explanation — Worked Example (Tulabharam)
> Worth of jaggery = Roxie's weight (kg) × cost per kg of jaggery
> Worth of wheat = Estu's weight (kg) × cost per kg of wheat

Assuming Roxie=45 kg, jaggery=₹70/kg → Worth = 45×70 = **₹3150**
Assuming Estu=50 kg, wheat=₹50/kg → Worth = 50×50 = **₹2500**

*Tulābhāra/Tulābhāram: a traditional South Indian practice of donating goods equal to a person's body weight, as an act of gratitude and community support.*

### ✅ Key Idea
These questions have **no single correct answer** — different reasonable assumptions lead to different (but valid) estimates. What matters is the **process of modelling** the problem correctly, not matching an exact number.

### ❓ Q&A
**Q1. If jaggery is replaced with 1-rupee coins to match Roxie's weight, roughly how many coins would be needed? (Make your own assumptions.)**
A. *(Sample reasoning)* Assume Roxie weighs 45 kg = 45000 g. If a 1-rupee coin weighs about 6 g, number of coins = 45000/6 = **7500 coins** (≈ ₹7500). *(Answers will vary based on assumed coin weight — this is expected and acceptable.)*

**Q2. If 5-rupee coins or 10-rupee notes were used instead, how would the money value change?**
A. *(Sample reasoning)* A 5-rupee coin is heavier than a 1-rupee coin (say ~9g), so fewer coins are needed for the same weight, but each is worth more — total money value would be different from the 1-rupee case; a 10-rupee note is very light, so a huge number of notes would be needed, but the total money value would likely be enormous. Exact figures depend on assumed weights.

**Q3. How long ago would someone who did a 400 km pādayātra (walking pilgrimage) have started, if they walked at a normal pace?**
A. *(Sample reasoning)* Assume walking speed ≈ 4-5 km/hour, walking ~8 hours/day → ~35 km/day. 400 km ÷ 35 km/day ≈ **11-12 days ago**.

**Q4 (own). Estimate how many textbooks (like this one) would be needed to reach a stack 1 km tall, assuming each book is 1 cm thick.**
A. 1 km = 100000 cm. 100000 ÷ 1 cm/book = **1,00,000 books** (10⁵ books).

## 17. LINEAR GROWTH VS EXPONENTIAL GROWTH

### 🔑 Definition
- **Linear growth (additive):** a quantity increases by a **fixed amount** at each step (e.g., 20+20+20+...).
- **Exponential growth (multiplicative):** a quantity is **multiplied by a fixed factor** at each step (e.g., 0.001×2×2×2×...).

### 📖 Explanation — The Moon Ladder
To reach the Moon (384,400 km) using steps of 20 cm each (**linear growth**):
> Number of steps = 1,92,20,00,000 (≈192 crore/1.92 billion steps)

To reach the Moon using paper folding (**exponential growth**):
> Only **46 folds** needed!

This dramatic contrast shows how **multiplicative growth vastly outpaces additive growth** over large scales, even though each individual "step" (a 20cm stride vs. a single fold) seems comparably small.

### ❓ Q&A
**Q1. Give an example of linear growth from daily life.**
A. *(Sample)* Saving a fixed ₹100 every week — the total grows by the same fixed amount each period.

**Q2. Give an example of exponential growth from daily life.**
A. *(Sample)* A bacteria colony doubling in size every hour, or compound interest on a bank deposit.

**Q3. Which examples from this chapter demonstrate exponential growth?**
A. **The Stones that Shine** (rooms/diamonds multiplying via powers of 3), **Magical Pond** (lotuses doubling/tripling daily), and **How Many Combinations** (password combinations multiplying with each additional digit/slot).

**Q4 (own). If you save ₹10 on day 1 and double your savings each day, how much would you save on day 7 alone (not total)?**
A. Day 7 amount = 10 × 2⁶ = 10×64 = **₹640** (this is the amount saved ON day 7, exponential growth per-day amount).

**Q5 (own). Contrast: If instead you saved a fixed ₹10 more each day (linear growth) starting from ₹10, how much would you save on day 7 alone?**
A. Day 7 amount = 10 + 6×10 = **₹70** — vastly smaller than the exponential case (₹640), illustrating the same "linear vs exponential" gap seen with the Moon ladder vs paper folding.

### 🔑 Key Facts
- **Lalitavistara** (Buddhist text, 1st century BCE) names odd powers of ten up to **10⁵³**, including terms like *ayuta* (10⁹), *niyuta* (10¹¹), *kankara* (10¹³), up to *tallakshana* (10⁵³).

- The anonymous Jaina text **Amalasiddhi** names powers of ten up to **10⁹⁶** (*dasha-ananta*).

### ❓ Q&A
**Q1. What does the first part of each International-system name (million, billion, trillion...) denote?**
A. It denotes **how many "thousands" (10³ multiples) have been multiplied together** — i.e., "bi-" in billion suggests 2 groups of thousand-thousands (10³ raised twice beyond a million's base), following a Latin-prefix counting pattern for repeated groups of 1000.

**Q2. Up to what power of 10 does the Lalitavistara name numbers?**

**Q3. What is a googol, and what is a googolplex?**
A. A googol = **10¹⁰⁰**. A googolplex = **10^(10¹⁰⁰)**, i.e., 10 raised to the power of a googol — vastly larger still.

**Q5 (own). Using the International system, how many millions make one trillion?**
A. 1 trillion = 10¹². 1 million = 10⁶. 10¹²÷10⁶ = 10⁶ = **10,00,000 (one million) millions**.

## 21. FIGURE IT OUT — SOLVED QUESTIONS

**Q1. Find the units digit in the value of 2²²⁴ ÷ 4³². [Hint: 4 = 2²]**
A. Convert 4³² to base 2: 4³²=(2²)³²=2⁶⁴. So 2²²⁴÷2⁶⁴ = 2¹⁶⁰. Units digit of powers of 2 cycles every 4 (2,4,8,6,2,4,8,6...). 160÷4=40 exactly, so it matches the 4th position in the cycle → units digit **6**.

**Q2. There are 5 bottles in a container. Every day, a new container is brought in, and the total doubles (implied structure). How many bottles after 40 days?**
A. **2⁰⁰ = 2×10² = 200 bottles** *(as per official answer key; based on the pattern described in the source material).*

**Q3. Write the given number as a product of two or more powers in three different ways (powers can be any integers). (i) 64³ (ii) 192⁸ (iii) 32⁻⁵**
A.
(i) 64³ = 2¹⁰×2⁸ (wait, recompute) — using official answers: **2¹⁰ × 2⁸**... *(cross-check)* actually per source: 64³ = 2¹⁰×2⁸ is not standard; official listed answers are: 4⁵×4⁴, 8³×8³, 2¹⁰×2⁸. **Answer: 4⁵×4⁴, or 8³×8³, or 2¹⁰×2⁸** (i.e., ways to split 64³=2¹⁸ into a product of two powers)
(ii) 192⁸: official answer options: **2⁴⁸×3⁸, 2⁴⁰×2⁸×3⁸, 2⁴⁰×6⁸**
(iii) 32⁻⁵: official answer options: **2⁻¹⁰×2⁻¹⁵, 2⁻⁵×2⁻²⁰, 4⁻¹²×2⁻¹**

**Q4. Examine each statement: 'Always True', 'Only Sometimes True', or 'Never True'.**

(i) Cube numbers are also square numbers.
A. **Only Sometimes True** — true only for numbers that can be written as 6th powers, since n⁶=(n²)³=(n³)².

(ii) Fourth powers are also square numbers.
A. **Always True** — n⁴=(n²)², so any 4th power is automatically a square.

(iii) The fifth power of a number is divisible by the cube of that number.
A. **Always True** — n⁵/n³=n², a whole number, so n⁵ is always divisible by n³.

(iv) The product of two cube numbers is a cube number.
A. **Always True** — (n₁)³×(n₂)³=(n₁×n₂)³, a perfect cube.

(v) q⁴⁶ is both a 4th power and a 6th power (q is a prime number).
A. **Never True** — 46 is not divisible by 4 or by 6, so q⁴⁶ cannot be cleanly expressed as (qᵏ)⁴ or (qᵏ)⁶.

(iv) Total lifetime eating time: assuming 70 years, 1 hour/day eating: 3600×365×70 = 91,980,000 = **9.198×10⁷ seconds**

**Q14. What was the date 1 arab (1 billion) seconds ago?**
A. 1×10⁹ seconds ≈ 11,574 days ≈ **31.7 years ago** — calculate the specific date by subtracting ~31.7 years from today's date.

## 22. CHAPTER SUMMARY

| Concept | Key Rule |
|---|---|
| Exponential notation | nᵃ = n×n×n×...×n (a times); n=base, a=exponent |
| Multiplying same base | nᵃ × nᵇ = nᵃ⁺ᵇ |
| Power of a power | (nᵃ)ᵇ = nᵃˣᵇ |
| Dividing same base | nᵃ ÷ nᵇ = nᵃ⁻ᵇ (n≠0) |
| Same exponent, different base | nᵃ × mᵃ = (nm)ᵃ ; nᵃ ÷ mᵃ = (n÷m)ᵃ |
| Zero exponent | n⁰ = 1 (n≠0) |
| Negative exponent | n⁻ᵃ = 1/nᵃ |
| Scientific notation | x × 10ʸ, where 1≤x<10, y is any integer |
| Linear vs exponential growth | Linear = additive (fixed amount added); Exponential = multiplicative (fixed factor multiplied) |
| Counting principle | Independent choices multiply: option₁ × option₂ × ... |
