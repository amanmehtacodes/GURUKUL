 Chapter-5                                            Speech
A speech is a formal address or discourse delivered to an audience. A speech seeks to convince, persuade, inspire or inform.
The main purpose of the speech is to convey the speaker’s message in such an effective way as may greatly inﬂuence the
audience. History is full of examples where speeches made by great orators brought about great revolutions. Mark Antony’s
speech in Shakespeare’s ‘Julius Caesar’ and Portia’s speech on mercy in Shakespeare’s ‘Merchant of Venice’, Satan’s
speech in John Milton’s ‘Paradise Lost’ are examples of some famous speeches in literature.
Great oratory has three components.
Style:        A great speech must be masterfully constructed. The best orators are masters of both the written and
              spoken word, and use words to create texts that are beautiful to both hear and read.
Substance: A speech may be ﬂowery and charismatically presented, and yet lack any true substance at all. Great
           oratory must centre on a worthy theme; it must appeal to and inspire the audience's ﬁnest values and
           ideals.
Impact:       Great oratory always seeks to persuade the audience of some fact or idea. The very best speeches
              change hearts and minds and seem as revelatory several decades or centuries removed as when they
              were ﬁrst given.

                                          Layout of a Speech
    1. Introduction:
            Æ Welcome, Greeting and Formal Address: First of all, the speaker is required to welcome, greet
                 and formally address the audience. The names of the addressees should be in correct sequence of
                 institutional hierarchy. For example: Address the Principal ﬁrst, and then teachers and fellow
                 students. Don't forget to mention the chief guests, dignitaries and other people who are a part of
                 the audience in the very beginning of your speech.
            Æ Introduce yourself telling your name, class, section and/or designation.
            Æ Introduce the topic or theme on which you are going to speak. You may introduce the topic by
                 sharing an anecdote, giving an example, citing a quotation, quoting some statistics in the very
                 beginning of your speech and then moving ahead with the rest of introduction.
    2. Body:
            Æ Provide information on various aspects of the theme you have selected to speak on. The
                 information may consist of arguments, examples, deﬁnitions, descriptions, comparisons,
                 quotations, facts supported with statistics etc.
            Æ Present your views in a logical sequence using appropriate cohesive devices and transitional
                 words and expressions.
    3. Conclusion:
            Æ Sum up your views and conclude your speech.
            Æ You may end by inspiring the audience to think and act in a particular way, warning the audience,
                 or by asking questions for the audience to ponder over or answer.
            Æ End with formal thanks by saying 'Thank you', 'Thank you everyone' or 'Thank you for the patient
                 hearing'.

                               Tips for Writing an Effective Speech
 1. Consider your audience.
 2. Collect information relevant to the topic or theme of your speech.
 3. Organise the information in a logical sequence and prepare a rough draft.
 4. Keep the time limit in mind.
 5. Keep the language conversational by using appropriate cohesive devices and by applying appropriate
    stress, tone and intonation. Use a variety of sentences: Afﬁrmative, Negative, Questions, Imperative,
    Exclamatory and Optative to make the speech as communicative as possible. Read the golden rules given
    below to add an extra pinch of spice to your speech.
 Æ Rule of 3: The human brain responds magically to things that come in threes. Whether it’s a list of
    adjectives, a joke, or your main points, it’s most effective if you keep it to this structure.
 Æ Imagery: Metaphors, similes and description will help an audience understand you, and keep them
    entertained.
 Æ Pronouns: Use “we” to create a sense of unity, “them” for a common enemy, “you” if you’re reaching out to
    your audience, and “I” / “me” if you want to take control.
 Æ Poetry: Repetition, rhyme and alliteration are sound effects, used by poets and orators alike. They make a
    speech much more memorable. Also remember to structure pauses and parentheses into a speech. This will
    vary the ﬂow of sound, helping you hold your audience’s attention.
 Æ Jokes: Humour is powerful. Use it to perk up a sleepy audience, as well as a rhetorical tool. Laughter is
    based on people having common, shared assumptions – and can therefore be used to persuade.
 6. Edit the rough draft and remove grammatical or spelling errors. Try to replace the same words with
    synonyms. Conclude effectively and urge the audience to take required action. End with a hope or positive
    statement.
 7. Thank your audience.

                                                        Speech Sample-1
           Swami Vivekananda’s Speech at the Chicago Convention of Parliament of Religions

      Sisters and Brothers of America,
      It ﬁlls my heart with joy unspeakable to rise in response to the warm and cordial welcome which you
      have given us. I thank you in the name of the most ancient order of monks in the world; I thank you in
      the name of the mother of religions, and I thank you in the name of millions and millions of Hindu
      people of all classes and sects.

      My thanks, also, to some of the speakers on this platform who, referring to the delegates from the
      Orient, have told you that these men from far-off nations may well claim the honour of bearing to
      different lands the idea of toleration. I am proud to belong to a religion which has taught the world both
      tolerance and universal acceptance. We believe not only in universal toleration, but we accept all
      religions as true. I am proud to belong to a nation which has sheltered the persecuted and the refugees of
      all religions and all nations of the earth. I am proud to tell you that we have gathered in our bosom the
      purest remnant of the Israelites, who came to Southern India and took refuge with us in the very year in
      which their holy temple was shattered to pieces by Roman tyranny. I am proud to belong to the religion
      which has sheltered and is still fostering the remnant of the grand Zoroastrian nation. I will quote to
      you, brethren, a few lines from a hymn which I remember to have repeated from my earliest boyhood,
      which is every day repeated by millions of human beings: "As the different streams having their
      sources in different paths which men take through different tendencies, various though they appear,
      crooked or straight, all lead to Thee."

      The present convention, which is one of the most august assemblies ever held, is in itself a vindication,
      a declaration to the world of the wonderful doctrine preached in the Geeta: "Whosoever comes to me,
      through whatsoever form, I reach him; all men are struggling through paths which in the end lead to
      me." Sectarianism, bigotry, and its horrible descendant, fanaticism, have long possessed this beautiful
      earth. They have ﬁlled the earth with violence, drenched it often and often with human blood, destroyed
      civilization and sent whole nations to despair. Had it not been for these horrible demons, human society
      would be far more advanced than it is now. But their time is come; and I fervently hope that the bell that
      tolled this morning in honour of this convention may be the death-knell of all fanaticism, of all
      persecutions with the sword or with the pen, and of all uncharitable feelings between persons wending
      their way to the same goal.

Write a speech on the topic ‘Importance of Discipline in Our Life’.

    Have you ever imagined what would happen if the earth stopped moving around the sun or the sun
    stopped giving heat and light to the world?

    Honourable Principal, worthy teachers and my dear friends. A very good morning to all of you. I am
    Ankita Sinha of grade VI A. Today I stand before you to express my views on the importance of
    discipline in our lives.

    Since the creation of this universe, the earth has been moving around the sun, the sun has been
    spreading its bright rays to one and all without any discrimination, the pole star has been guiding the
    travellers and the trees have been providing us Oxygen. All the objects, living or non-living maintain a
    strict discipline. Life would be chaotic in the absence of discipline. To be disciplined is to submit to a
    good rule or system, to maintain certain restraints for ultimate general good. If discipline is violated,
    steps must be taken against the offender.

    Discipline is necessary in all spheres of life. An army cannot ﬁght without strict discipline. An
    educational institution cannot run without discipline. Teachers and students must come to the school at
    a ﬁxed hour every day, and observe the rules of the institution. Courts, ofﬁces, auditoriums, and even
    private houses have to maintain discipline. One cannot go into a hospital at hours when visitors are not
    allowed. One has to stand in a queue to pay bills for electricity or telephone, or for buying tickets for
    cinema or train journey.

    In personal life, too, one has to practice discipline for a stable and worthy life. If you give in to
    temptations and overeat yourself, the stomach will revolt. If you watch TV throughout night, you will
    get sick the next day. If you do not restrain yourself from music and story-books before the
    examinations, you are sure to fare ill. Similarly, one cannot enjoy any sport if one is not disciplined as
    discipline is the very soul of any sport.

    In fact, life is best enjoyed only if discipline is maintained. Nothing can be properly enjoyed, not even a
    game of cricket or a musical concert, if indiscipline is allowed to invade it. Therefore, we should all try
    to maintain discipline in our lives.

    Thank you. Have a great day!
