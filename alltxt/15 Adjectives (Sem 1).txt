Adjectives of quality describe the kind, quality or characteristics of a noun or a pronoun. These adjectives
describe size, colour, shape, state, age, opinion, material etc. of a noun or a pronoun.
Examples: Red bangles, circular motion, huge ship, old memories, interesting story, common name, silver
lining, etc.
Proper Adjectives: Adjectives which are formed from proper nouns are called proper adjectives.
Examples: Indian Flag, Chinese toys, British Airways, American author etc.

                                                   Adjectives of Number

An adjectives of number, as the name suggests, describes the number of a noun or a pronoun. It answers
the question, 'how many'. It is used for countable nouns or pronouns.
There are two types of adjectives of number.
(i) Deﬁnite Numeral Adjectives: Deﬁnite numeral adjectives indicate the exact number of a noun or a pronoun.
Example: Two boys are playing in the park.
There are two types of deﬁnite numeral adjectives.
(a) Cardinals: Cardinals denote the exact number of a noun or a pronoun.
Example: There are ﬁve boys in this row.
(b) Ordinals: Ordinals denote the order or rank of a noun or a pronoun. Examples: ﬁrst, second, third, tenth, last,
next etc. Article 'the' or a possessive determiner is used before an ordinal.
Example: The ﬁfth boy in this row is my friend.
(ii) Indeﬁnite Numeral Adjectives: Indeﬁnite numeral adjective doesn’t indicate the exact number of the noun
or pronoun it refers to.
Example: Several people are waiting in the queue.
           There are many birds on this tree.
                                                  Adjectives of Quantity
An adjective of quantity indicates the quantity of a noun or a pronoun. It answers the question, 'how
much'. It is used for uncountable nouns or pronouns.
Examples: some, any, no, much, little, half, all, whole, sufﬁcient, enough, great, etc.
   1. I ate some rice.
   2. There is not much oil in the bottle.
   3. I don't have any money.
   4. This statement makes no sense.
   5. There is sufﬁcient milk in the jug.
   6. I don't have enough time to waste on these matters.

                                        Possessive Adjectives

The words 'my', 'our', 'your', 'his', 'her', 'its' and 'their' are used as possessive adjectives.
Examples: 1. India is our country.
              2. You must complete your work.
              3. It is her notebook.
              4. The dog wagged its tail.
                    Possessive Pronouns and Possessive Adjectives
Both possessive pronouns and possessive adjectives are used to show possession or belonging. However,
whereas pronouns replace the nouns, adjectives describe the nouns. Possessive adjectives are placed before
nouns. Examples:
       Possessive Pronoun                                   Possessive Adjective
       This book is mine.                                   This is my book.
       That house is yours.                                 That is your house.
       These bags are theirs                                These are their bags.
       This pencil is hers.                                 This is her pencil.
Important Note: Don’t use any apostrophe (’s) with possessive adjectives. The only exception is the pronoun
‘one’ which takes an apostrophe (’s) to make its possessive adjective. Example: One must do one’s duty.
                                   Demonstrative Adjectives
The words 'this', 'that', 'these', 'those' and 'such' are called demonstratives as they are used to point out to
persons or things they refer to.
Look at the use of demonstrative Adjectives in the following sentences.
  1. This pencil is mine. (Used for singular thing which is near the speaker)
  2. That car is new. (Used for singular thing which is at a distance from the speaker)
  3. These kites are mine. (Used for plural things which are near the speaker)
  4. Those oranges are fresh . (Used for plural things which are at a distance from the speaker)
  5. I don't like such people.
            Demonstrative Pronouns and Demonstrative Adjectives
Both demonstrative pronouns and demonstrative adjectives are used to point out a noun or a pronoun they refer
to. However, whereas pronouns replace the nouns, adjectives describe the nouns. Examples:
      Demonstrative Pronoun                             Demonstrative Adjective
      This is my book.                                  This book is mine.
      That is your house.                               That house is yours.
      These are their bags.                             These bags are theirs.
      This is her pencil.                               This pencil is hers.
                                    Interrogative Adjectives
The words ‘what’, ‘which’ and ‘whose’ are interrogative pronouns used to ask questions about persons or
things. These words can be used as interrogative adjectives too. When used just before a noun, these words
become interrogative adjectives.
                 Interrogative Pronoun                            Interrogative Adjective
      Which of these is your book?                      Which book is yours?
      What is the news he broke to you?                 What news did he break to you?
      Whose is this house?                              Whose house is this?

                                          Distributive Adjectives
The adjectives that refer to a single person or thing in a group, one at a time, are called distributive
adjectives. ‘each’, ‘every’, ‘either’, ‘neither’ are called distributive adjectives. Nouns preceded by
distributive adjectives always take a singular verb.
                                                Each and Every
‘Each’ and ‘every’ are similar in meaning, but there is a difference between the two. Each is used when speaking
of two or more persons or things. 'Every' is used when speaking of more than two persons or things. Each directs
attention to every person or thing of a group individually and separately. 'Every' is comprehensive, considering
all; it directs attention to the whole group without any exception. Each is used to denote a person or thing out of a
known or limited group. 'Every' is used in a wide sense to denote persons or things when the number is unlimited.
Examples: 1. Each child was given a sweet. (considering each individual out of a limited group)
                   2. Every child likes sweets. (considering all in an unlimited group)

                                             Either and Neither
'Either' has two meanings: (i) One out of two (ii) Each of two
'Neither' means None out of two.
Examples:       1. There are trees on either side of the road. (each out of two)
                2. Neither side of the road has trees. (None out of two)

                  Distributive Pronouns and Distributive Adjectives
                    Distributive Pronoun                                      Distributive Adjective
     1. Each of the students was given a prize.                    1. Each student was given a prize.
     2. Either of the two boys had completed his work.             2. Either boy completed his work.
     3. Neither of the sides of road has trees.                    3. Neither side of the road has trees.
                                         Emphasizing Adjectives
    Emphasizing adjectives lay stress on a particular person or thing.
    Examples:
       1. His very name makes me smile. 2. He is making a pot with his own hands.
                                         Exclamatory Adjectives
    Exclamatory adjectives are used before nouns in an exclamatory sentence.
    Examples:
       1. What a beautiful house! 2. What good luck!

                                                   Degrees of Adjectives
      There are three degrees of adjectives.
      (i) Positive Degree: A positive degree of adjective is used when the adjective describes only one thing or
      person without making any comparison. Even if there is any comparison involved, it is between one and
      none.
      Examples: Ram is a tall boy. (Describing only Ram)
                   No boy in the class is as tall as Ram.( Comparison between one and non)

                                                                       104
   (ii) Comparative Degree: A comparative degree of adjective is used when the adjective compares two
   persons, things or groups. Here, the mode of comparison is 'one to one'. The comparative degree is followed by
   the conjunction 'than'. The comparison may be made in three ways.
   (a) Individual compared with individual Example: Ram is taller than Shyam.
   (b) Individual compared with a group Example: Ram is taller than any other boy in the class.
   (c) Group compared with a group Example: The players of team A are taller than the players of team B.
   (iii) Superlative Degree: A superlative degree of adjective is used when one person or thing is compared with
   all others in the group. Here the mode of comparison is 'one to all' Example: Ram is the tallest boy in the class.

The present participle form and the past participle form of the verb can also be used as an adjective. These are
called verbal adjectives.

Examples:
1. I have a dancing doll.
2. The movie was interesting.
3. We found the house locked.
4. The lost child was crying bitterly.
In the examples give above, the verbs 'dancing' and 'interesting' are the present participle forms of the verbs
describing the nouns 'doll' and 'movie' respectively. Similarly, the verbs 'locked' and 'lost are the past
participle forms of the verbs describing the nouns 'house and 'child' respectively.

             Transformation of Sentences by using Degrees of Adjectives
Read the following three sentences carefully and observe the way of transformation.
1. No boy in the class is as intelligent as Ram.
2. Ram is more intelligent than any other boy in the class.
3. Ram is the most intelligent boy in the class.
The meaning of all the three sentences written above is the same. In all of them, it is Ram who is the most
intelligent boy. He is the only boy who is the most intelligent. However, this sense is conveyed by using Positive,
Comparative and Superlative Degrees of the Adjectives.
Read the following three sentences carefully and observe the way of transformation.
1. Few boys in the class are as intelligent as Ram.
2. Ram is more intelligent than most other boys in the class.
3. Ram is one of the most intelligent boys in the class.
The meaning of all the three sentences written above is the same. In all of them, Ram is one of the most intelligent
boys in the class. He is not the only boy who is the most intelligent. However, this sense is conveyed by using
Positive, Comparative and Superlative Degree of the Adjectives.
Read the following sentences carefully and observe the way of transformation.
1. Ram is not the tallest of all the boys in the class.
2. Ram is not taller than some other boys in the class.
3. Some other boys in the class are at least as tall as Ram.
Read the following sentence carefully.
Ritika is not as beautiful as Rashmi.
In this sentence, there is a comparison between two people. The same sentence can be rewritten without changing
the sense in the following way.
Rashmi is more beautiful than Ritika.
Read the following sentence carefully.
Abdul is as strong as his brother.
In this sentence, there is a comparison between two people. The same sentence can be rewritten without changing
the sense in the following way.
Abdul's brother is not stronger than he is.
