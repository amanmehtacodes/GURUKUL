 Chapter-13                                                              Tenses
Read the following sentences carefully.
    1. I was a child a few years ago.
    2. I am a youngster now.
    3. I shall be old in future.
The above three sentences refer to a state of being or existence at three different points of time. The verb 'was'
refers to existence in the past. The verb 'am' refers to existence in the present. The verb 'shall be' refers to existence
at some time in future.
Let's take some more examples.
    1. He wore a pink shirt yesterday.
    2. He is wearing a black shirt today.
    3. He will wear a white shirt tomorrow.
The verbs 'wore', 'is wearing' and 'will wear' in the above sentences refer to actions in the past, present and future
time respectively.
The word 'tense' has been derived from the Latin word 'tempus' which means time. The tense is a verb
form that shows the time of action or event. It also indicates the state of an action.
There are three Tenses.
    1. Present Tense
    2. Past Tense
    3. Future Tense
                                                       Sub Divisions of Tenses
Each tense is further sub-divided in four tenses according to the time of actions they denote. These sub divisions
are:
     1. Indeﬁnite or Simple: A verb in simple or indeﬁnite tense denotes an action that does not refer to any
        speciﬁc time.
     2. Continuous: A verb in continuous tense denotes an action in progress.
     3. Perfect: A verb in perfect tense denotes a complete action.
     4. Perfect Continuous: A verb in perfect continuous tense denotes an action in progress for some time.
Study the table given below to understand the division of each tense. The table shows all the tense forms of the
verb ‘write’.

      Simple                 He writes a letter.                   He wrote a letter                       He will write a letter.

                                        Use of Simple Present Tense
1. To express universal truths and facts such as a scientiﬁc fact, mathematical fact, geographical fact etc:
  Æ The earth revolves around the sun.
  Æ Water boils at 100 degree centigrade.
  Æ Two and two make four.
2. To express a habitual action, routines, customs, traditions etc:
   Æ He always speaks the truth.
   Æ People celebrate Diwali to mark the return of Lord Rama from exile.
   Æ Most men in the Punjab wear turbans.
   Æ My mother goes to temple every day.
3. To express proverbial statements:
   Æ Honesty is the best policy.
   Æ Riches have wings.
   Æ Early to bed and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy and wise.
4. For live commentaries:
   Æ Rajeev passes the ball to Nitish, Nitish passes it to Arjun who heads towards the goal and scores a goal.
5. To denote a planned future action:
   Æ Our school reopens next week.
   Æ The match begins the next hour.
6. In news paper headlines:
   Æ Cricket match between Srilanka and England ends in a draw.
   Æ Peace talk between India and Pakistan fails.
7. To quote poets or authors whether living or dead:
  Æ Shakespeare says, “Sweet are the uses of adversity.”
8. In subordinate clauses (starting with if, in case, provided that, unless, till, until, as soon as, when, before
   etc.)showing time or condition when the main clause is in the future:( type-1 conditional sentences)
   Æ If it rains, we will not go out.
   Æ When my father comes home, we shall play chess.

                                       Use of Present Continuous Tense
1. To denote an action in progress at the time of speaking:
   Æ I am going to school.
   Æ The boys are playing cricket in the stadium.
   Æ My mother is cooking food.
2. To denote an action in progress and continuity around present time, not necessarily at the time of
   speaking:
   Æ I am learning French these days.
   Æ India is progressing by leaps and bounds.
   Æ She is writing a book.
3. Planned future actions:
   Æ We are going to play a cricket match this evening.
   Æ They are meeting tonight.
4. Use of 'will' and 'be going to' for future actions
   Both 'will' and 'be going to' are used to denote future actions. However, there is a difference between the
   two. We use 'will' when we plan to do something at the time of speaking. On the other hand, 'be going to'
   is used to denote actions that are planned or actions that are likely to happen.
   Æ My friend is going to get married. (planned action)
   Æ She will get married after her Ph.D. (not planned as yet)
   Æ Sumit is riding his bicycle carelessly. He is going to fall. (action likely to happen)
   Æ Look at the clouds in the sky. It's going to rain.
   Æ I don't have money to buy a car. I will take a loan from bank.
   Æ I am going to take loan from bank. (already planned) or, I am taking loan from bank.

                                  Use of Present Perfect Continuous Tense
   1. To denote an action that started in the past and continues in present:
           Æ It has been raining since morning.
           Æ They have been waiting for the chief guest for one hour.
   2. For repeated actions when the number of repetitions are not mentioned:
           Æ I have been telling him not to ﬂy kites. (frequency not mentioned)
           Æ I have told him many times not to ﬂy kites. (Frequency mentioned using the word 'many')
   3. Drawing conclusions:
           Æ The roads are ﬂooded. It seems that it has been raining for long.
           Æ He is tired. It looks that he has been working continuously.
   4. To ask questions starting with 'How long' or 'Since when':
           Æ How long have you been living in this city?
           Æ Since when have you been serving in this ofﬁce?
