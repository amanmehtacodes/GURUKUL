Chapter-14              Agreement of the Verb with Subject
Read the following sentences carefully.
   1. Ram and Sham is friends.
   2. I were not there.
   3. He like mangoes.
   4. All the boys is playing cricket.
   5. You is my friend.
   6. They has taken their meal.
 THINK: Do you notice any abnormality in these sentences?
In each sentence given above, the verb does not agree with the subject in number or person. Now read
the same sentences again.
    1. Ram and Sham are friends.
    2. I was not there.
    3. He likes mangoes.
    4. All the boys are playing cricket.
    5. You are my friend.
    6. They have taken their meal.
A ﬁnite verb agrees with its subject in number, person or tense. This is known as subject-verb agreement or
agreement of the verb with its subject.
Read the following rules and examples carefully to understand subject-verb agreement.
   1. A singular subject takes a singular verb and a plural subject takes a plural verb.
       Examples:
       (a) The baby is sleeping.                      (d) They read a book.
       (b) The babies are sleeping.                   (e) I am waiting for you.
       (c) He reads a book.                           (f) We are waiting for you.
   2. When two or more nouns or pronouns are joined by 'and', and they represent two separate persons
       or things, the verb used should be plural.
       Examples:
       (a) Seeta and Geeta are going to market.       (c) You and he have taken your lunch.
       (b) Gold and Platinum are precious metals. (d) Time and tide wait for none.
   3. When two or more nouns or pronouns are joined by 'and', but they refer to or represent a single
       persons, thing or concept, the verb should be singular.
       Examples:
       (a) Early to bed and early to rise is a good habit. (b) Rice and curry is my favourite meal.
       (c) The horse and carriage is ready.                  (d) My friend, guide and philosopher is my father.
   4. When two or more singular subjects are connected by 'or' or 'nor' the verb should be singular.
       Examples: (a) Either Kunal or his friend has broken the window,
                    (b) Neither the milkman or his son is ploughing the ﬁeld.
   5. When a singular and a plural subject is connected by 'or' or 'nor', the plural subject is kept near the
       verb and the verb agrees with the plural subject.
       Examples: (a) Either Kunal or his friends have broken the window.
                    (b) Neither the milkman or his sons are ploughing the ﬁeld.
   6. When the subjects joined by 'or' or 'nor' are pronouns of different persons, the verb should agree
       with the pronoun that is nearer the verb.
       Examples:
       (a) Either you or I am to blame.               (b) Neither you nor he is mistaken.

      7. When a Noun or a Pronoun is joined with another noun (s) or pronoun (s) using the words, 'with', 'as well as,
          'along with', 'no less than', 'together with', 'besides' or 'and not', the verb agrees with the one introduced
          ﬁrst in the sentence.
      Examples:
         (a) The coach, with all the players, is practising in the ﬁeld.
         (b) The players, as well as the coach, are practising in the ﬁeld.
         (c) Abhinav, no less than his colleagues, is very honest.
         (d) Geetika, and not her sisters, has baked the cake.
         (e) I, as well as they, am sick of his behaviour.
      8. The words such as 'each', 'every', 'either', 'neither', 'many a', 'everyone', 'everybody', 'somebody'
          'one of', 'none of' etc. take a singular verb.
          Examples: (a) Each child was given a candy.           (b) Everybody wants to be appreciated.
                          (c) Somebody has stolen my purse. (d) One of my friends has settled in the USA.
                          (e) Many a student was playing in the playground.
      9. If two singular subjects joined by 'and' are qualiﬁed by 'each' or 'every', they take a singular verb.
          Examples: (a) Each hour and each minute is precious.
                          (b) Every man and every woman desires happiness.
      10. Error of Proximity: When the subject is a phrase, the verb must agree with the subject proper
          instead of the word which is near the verb. The error of making the verb agree with the word near it
          is called the error of proximity.
          Examples: (a) The attitude of the ofﬁcers is friendly. (b) A list of the candidates was prepared.
      11. Some nouns which appear to be singular, but are treated as plural, take a plural verb.
          Examples: (a) The cattle are grazing in the ﬁeld.            (b) Six dozen cost rupees ﬁve hundred.
                          (c) The people were shouting.
      12. Some nouns appear to be plural, but they take a singular verb.
          Examples: (a) Politics is a dirty game.                      (b) The news of his arrival has come.
                          (c) The wages of sin is death.               (d) Mathematics is my favourite subject.
      13. Collective nouns like committee, government, jury, panel etc. take singular verb when these nouns
          are used in the sense of a single unit or one whole; and plural verb when the individuals making the
          whole are considered separately.
          Examples: (a) The jury was unanimous in its stand on the issue.
                          (b) The jury were divided on this controversial issue.

      14. If a plural noun is used as a name of some singular object or collective unit, the verb that follows will
         be singular.
         Examples:       (a) The Arabian Nights was translated by Richard Burton.
                         (b) Gulliver's Travels is an interesting book.
      15. When a plural noun denoting some speciﬁc quantity or amount is taken in the sense of a whole, the
          verb it takes should be generally singular. If it is not taken as a single whole, it takes a plural verb.
          Examples: (a) Eight months is a long time.                 (b) Fifty miles is a long distance.
                         (c) Five thousand rupees is a great sum. (d) Five thousand rupees were paid to the
          workers.
      16. When two nouns joined by 'and' take a single article, they refer to the same person and take a singular
          verb, when they take two separate articles; they refer to two persons and take a plural verb.
          Examples: (a) The poet and novelist was honoured.
                         (b) The poet and the novelist were honoured.
      17. Certain phrases such as, 'a lot of', 'a plenty of', 'some of', 'most of', 'half of', 'all the' etc. take a
          singular verb if followed by a singular noun; a plural verb if followed by a plural noun.
      Examples:          (a) A lot of money was wasted on this project.
                         (b) A lot of books were scattered in the room.
