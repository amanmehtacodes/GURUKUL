Read the following sentences carefully with special focus on the function of the italicised words.

Adverb: The word that modiﬁes or adds to the meaning of a verb, an adjective or another adverb is called
an adverb
                                          Types of Adverbs
                                         1. Adverb of Time
An adverb of time denotes the time of the action or verb in a sentence. It answers the question 'when'.
Examples: soon, early, late, yesterday, today, still, tomorrow, ago, already, just, before etc.
  Æ They haven't seen a zoo before.
  Æ You can go home now.
  Æ She is leaving for Delhi tomorrow.
                                         2. Adverb of Place
An adverb of place denotes the place of the action or verb in a sentence. It answers the question ‘where’.
Examples: above, up, below, down, far, near, within, without, away, out, outside, inside, in etc.
  Æ Don’t sit here.
  Æ I will not go there.
  Æ He could not ﬁnd his book anywhere
                                       3. Adverb of Manner
An adverb of manner denotes the way or manner of the action or verb in a sentence. It answers the
question ‘how’.
Examples: fast, hurriedly, beautifully, speedily, correctly, rightly, boldly, madly etc.
  Æ Laxmibai fought bravely in the battle against the British.
  Æ A tortoise walks slowly.
  Æ He performed well at the function.
                                4. Adverb of Degree or Extent
An adverb of degree or extent denotes the extent or limit of the verb, adjective or some other adverb in a
sentence. It answers the question ‘how much or ‘to what extent’.
Examples: too, very, extremely, almost, pretty, far, fairly, nearly, quite, enough, much, fully, partly, rather,
so, little, much, too etc.
   Æ The apples are very sweet.
   Æ He is quite well now.
   Æ The work of construction of my house is almost complete now.
                             5. Adverb of Frequency or Number
An adverb of frequency or number denotes the number of times of the action or verb in a sentence. It
answers the question ‘how many times’ or ‘how often’.
Examples: once, twice, thrice, always, often, usually, seldom, ﬁrstly, secondly, frequently, again, sometimes,
etc.
   Æ He called you up twice.
   Æ He has always supported me.
   Æ She is often in a bad mood.

                                 6. Adverb of Affirmation or Negation
An adverb of afﬁrmation denotes the positivity or certainty of the action or verb in a sentence.
Examples: yes, surely, deﬁnitely, certainly, of course, etc.
  Æ He will deﬁnitely help you.
  Æ She will surely submit the report tomorrow.
An Adverb of Negation denotes the negativity of the action/verb in a sentence.
Examples: not, never, hardly, rarely, scarcely, seldom
  Æ He will not help you.
  Æ She never comes late.

                                             7. Interrogative Adverbs
The words 'when', 'where', 'how', 'why' are called interrogative adverbs as they are used to
ask questions.
Examples:
   1. When are you going to Delhi?                                         3. How did you solve the sum?
   2. Where have you kept my shoes?                                        4. Why have you not taken your lunch?

When an interrogative adverb is used to join two clauses and refers to some antecedent in the principal
clause, it is called relative adverb. Observe carefully how the following pair of sentences is joined using a
relative adverb.
I know a company. He works there.
I know the company where he works.
Examples:
        1. I don’t know the time when he will return.
        2. There must be some reason why he has left the job.
        3. Can you show how this machine is operated?
        4. My brother told me how the sum could be solved.

                                                                  Forms of Adverbs
     Æ Some adverbs and adjectives have the same form but are used differently.

                         Adjective                                                                                   Adverb
        She speaks in a loud voice.                                                Don’t speak so loud.
        He is a fast runner .                                                      He runs fast.
        They have much work to do.                                                 She is not much interested in this job.
        They work hard.                                                            She is a hard worker.
     Æ Some words may function as different types of adverbs.
       He was sitting far from me. (Adverb of place)
       My painting is far more beautiful than yours. (Adverb of degree)
     Æ Some adverbs have two forms having different meanings.
       She works very hard. (diligently)               She hardly works. (scarcely)
       They found a hotel quite near. (not far away)   She was nearly unconscious. (almost)
                                                            Formation of Adverbs
Adverbs are formed in the following ways.
   1. By adding '-ly' to various parts of speech
      Examples: forcefully, cleverly, monthly, amazingly, hurriedly

                                                  Comparison of Adverbs
Adverbs can be used to compare verbs, adjectives or adverbs. Like adjectives, adverbs too have three degrees of
comparison: Positive, Comparative and Superlative. The three degrees of adverbs are formed in the following
manners.

1. By adding ‘-er’ and ‘-est’ to the Positive Degree:
            Positive Degree           Comparative Degree                                           Superlative Degree
                   near                         nearer                                                    nearest
                   late                          later                                                     latest
2. By adding ‘more’ and ‘most’ before the positive degree:
              Positive Degree                           Comparative Degree                          Superlative Degree
                  beautifully                             more beautifully                            most beautifully
                  intelligently                           more intelligently                          most intelligently

                  Positive Degree           Comparative Degree             Superlative Degree

             Transformation of Sentences by removing the adverb ‘too’
(I) If the adverb 'too' is followed by an adjective and then by a verb in the inﬁnitive mood, the sentence is
converted into a complex sentence with two clauses- the ﬁrst clause containing 'so' and the second beginning
with 'that'.
Example:
This trunk is too heavy for me to lift.
This trunk is so heavy that I cannot lift it.
He is too weak to walk.
He is so weak that he cannot walk.


                                                                       82
(ii) If the original sentence contains no negative word, the subordinate clause becomes negative. If the
sentence contains a negative word, the subordinate clause becomes positive. Example
     1. He is too clever to be easily deceived. (positive)
           He is so clever that he cannot be easily deceived. (Negative)
     2. He is too conﬁdent not to face the interview. (Negative)
           He is so conﬁdent that he will face the interview. (Positive)
(iii) If there is no inﬁnitive after too, it can be replaced by the word more than enough, beyond the proper
limit, over, excessively, very, extremely etc.
Examples:
     He is too eager for praise.                            He is over eager for praise.
     He is too intelligent.                                 He is very intelligent.
     This city is too crowded.                              This city is crowded beyond the limit.
